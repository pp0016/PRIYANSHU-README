> **Classification:** Channel Operating Standard (Permanent, Internal)
> **Version:** 1.0 — Compiled 2026-10-18
> **Source Corpus:** 12 scripts (6 outliers, 6 flops) forensically analyzed across 3 independent vidIQ sessions, cross-referenced with the Script-System skill (21 locked rules)
> **One-Line Thesis:** *"The outliers made the viewer the protagonist of a financial system they did not know they were inside; the flops explained a system to the viewer from the outside."*

> [!CAUTION]
> **This gate has no soft exits.** There are exactly three permitted verdicts: `PASS`, `REJECT — PREMISE`, or `REJECT — DRAFT`. The phrases "pass with notes," "good enough for this week," and "we'll fix it in the edit" are permanently banned. A gate with a soft exit is not a gate.

## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

**If you are not a scriptwriter** — and you're reading this for the first time — here's what you need to know:

1. **A quality gate is a checkpoint system.** Think of it like airport security: your script must pass through every scanner in order. If it fails at any point, it goes back — it doesn't get "almost through."

2. **Why this matters for your channel:** The difference between an outlier video (100K+ views) and a flop (75 views) is almost never the topic. It's almost always the *execution.* This gate catches execution failures before you spend money on voiceover, animation, and publishing.

3. **The two types of failure:**
   - **Premise Failure** = the topic itself is wrong (a personal challenge instead of a system reveal). *Fix: Choose a different topic.*
   - **Delivery Failure** = the topic is fine but the script broke immersion, announced structure, or lacked stakes. *Fix: Keep the topic, rebuild the script from the beat sheet.* This is the critical insight: Flop 6 scored **9/10 on title alignment** and still died — because delivery killed it.

4. **The stages run in strict order.** Never skip to scoring (Stage 3) without clearing the kill triggers (Stage 1) first. A script with a kill trigger cannot be rescued by good prose.

5. **Never gate your own script in the same session that wrote it.** An author cannot find its own blind spots. A model once graded its own flawed script 9.5/10.

## STAGE 0 — TOPIC AUTHORIZATION

> **Philosophy:** *"A script that was never worth writing cannot be rescued by good prose. Stage 0 confirms the topic was authorised, not improvised."*

A topic may be commissioned **only if it passes ALL tests below.** If it fails any single test, it is rejected at intake — before a single line of script is written.

### The 7 Authorization Tests

| # | Test Name | Question | Pass Condition | Fail Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0.1** | **The Mechanism Test** | Does the topic expose a hidden or counter-intuitive mechanism of wealth, power, or social dynamics? | A named *system*, not a behaviour | "No-buy year" (a personal challenge, not a system) |
| **0.2** | **The Ladder Test** | Does the topic contain a countable progression so the script has a built-in spine? | Ages, levels, wealth tiers, or thresholds that create natural staircase units | "5 Expenses I Cut" (5 unconnected items, no progression) |
| **0.3** | **The Invisible-Cost Test** | Does the topic have a price that is NOT denominated in money? | The payoff must include cost in conversation, relationships, silence, time, or identity | "Save $4000" (payoff is only dollars) |
| **0.4** | **The Second-Person Test** | Can the topic be written as "you" without lying? | The honest version uses "you," not "many people" or "I" | "The Year *I* Stopped Replacing Boredom" |
| **0.5** | **The Send Test** | Can you draft one line a viewer would text to someone? | Write that line during intake or reject the topic | Outlier 4: *"You have not stood in a line in longer than you can accurately remember."* |
| **0.6** | **The Secret Test** | Is there a "secret" or "unseen" element the script unveils? | Answerable in one sentence | "How Banks Treat You" (hidden tiers most don't know exist) |
| **0.7** | **The Stranger Test** | Would a stranger with no interest in you personally still want the answer? | The topic stands without the creator | Personal diary entries fail this |

### Topics to Refuse on Sight
- Anything titled as a numbered list of tips
- Anything whose premise is "I did X"
- Anything requiring "should" or "need to" in the hook
- Anything where the payoff is a rule the viewer must follow
- Self-help challenges, personal budgeting experiments, or abstract concept discussions
- Any topic that is a person's behaviour rather than a system's mechanism

### The 3 Mandatory Topic Questions (All must be answerable in 1 sentence)
1. *"Whose unspoken struggle is this?"*
2. *"What is the journey from/to?"*
3. *"What does the viewer suspect that we are about to confirm?"*

### Pre-Write Checklist (Must Tick Before Writing Line 1)
- [ ] Topic passes all 7 authorization tests
- [ ] There is a secret (nameable in one sentence)
- [ ] There is a "you" (not "people," not "I")
- [ ] Opening image is physical (time + place + body)
- [ ] Stakes have a date (deadline or countdown)
- [ ] Numbers have owners (damages or protects someone)
- [ ] Tension refreshes at least twice
- [ ] Ending re-reads the opening image
- [ ] Cognitive contract is written down before draft one
- [ ] WPM is measured (from ElevenLabs with channel voice), NOT assumed
- [ ] Word budget is calculated: `RUNTIME (min) × measured WPM (±5%)`
- [ ] Hook cap is calculated: `Hook seconds × WPM ÷ 60`
- [ ] Hook type is chosen from the rotation (not the same as the last 3 videos)

### WPM Resolution (Resolving the 150 vs 200 Conflict)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Never assume a speaking pace.** File 2 pins 150 WPM (documentary pace). Your channel brief says 200 WPM. The script-system skill says "measure it." The correct answer:
>
> 1. **Priority 1 — Measure from ElevenLabs:** Paste ~200 words into ElevenLabs with your channel's voice and settings. `words ÷ audio duration in minutes` = your actual WPM.
> 2. **Priority 2 — Measure from a reference:** `transcript words ÷ runtime` of an outlier in your niche.
> 3. **Priority 3 — 150 WPM default** ONLY if neither exists, with an explicit warning that the budget may be wrong.
>
> **Use your measured WPM for ALL calculations in this gate.** Update this section once measured.

## STAGE 1 — INSTANT DISQUALIFICATION TRIGGERS

> **Execution Rule:** Scan the script once for these. If **any single trigger fires**, the script is **REJECTED** and the gate stops. Do not proceed to Stage 2. Do not tally points. There is no compensation mechanism.

### A. Opening Kills (Triggers 1–5)

| Code | Trigger | What It Looks Like | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1** | The script opens on a negation of something the viewer hasn't been told | *"I don't follow those people. I don't engage with that content."* — viewer has no referent | Flop 3 |
| **D2** | The script opens with "Picture this" or asks the viewer to construct the scene | *"Picture this. Every month you pay taxis appears before the 15th."* | Flop 4 |
| **D3** | The script shifts from immersion to lecture in the opening | *"It was in 2018, and it started on January 1st, 2018..."* — autobiography, not immersion | Flop 5 |
| **D4** | The script introduces abstract concepts or rules before a concrete problem | *"One of the rules is the wouldn't repurchase rule... a spiritual cousin of the spontaneous combustion rule"* | Flop 1 |
| **D5** | The opening figure is never reconciled later in the script | *"€300,500 a month"* never returns; closing figure collapses to *"I had saved 1,000"* | Flop 4 |

### B. Perspective Kills (Triggers 6–9)

|---|---|---|---|

| **D9** | The script's subject is a person's behaviour rather than a system's mechanism | The no-buy year as a personal challenge | Flop 5 |

### C. Structure Kills (Triggers 10–14)

| Code | Trigger | What It Looks Like | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D10** | The script announces its own structure ("step one," "next," "point two") | *"Step two, create rules you can't break"* | Flop 4 |
| **D11** | The script is built as a taxonomy, ladder, or numbered tier list | *"Level one, negative balance. Level two, low balance..."* announced as headers | Flop 6 |
| **D12** | Financial information delivered as a category list with figures | *"Bars and beers with friends, 150 a month. Cigarettes, another 100."* | Flop 4 |

| **D14** | Act-percentage scaffolding or a percentage table in the outline | Real outlier scripts are topic-driven, not formula-locked | Original constraint |

### D. Tone & Stakes Kills (Triggers 15–19)

| Code | Trigger | What It Looks Like | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D15** | The script tells the viewer to do something (imperative instruction mid-story) | *"Want something? Write it down and wait 30 days."* | Flop 4 |
| **D16** | The script states or implies nothing is at stake | *"If this becomes miserable, I'll stop. I'm an adult."* / *"I don't need to do this. Nobody's making me."* | Flop 3 |

| **D18** | Disclaimer cascade that denies its own premise | *"This isn't about deprivation... I'm not trying to punish myself... This isn't about judging anyone else."* | Flop 3 |

| Code | Trigger | What It Looks Like | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

| **D21** | CTA stack (like / subscribe / comment / share requests) | *"smash that like button, subscribe for more straight talk finance, and drop your worst money wasting habit in the comments below"* | Flop 4 |
| **D22** | The ending fails to resolve the opening contract, or ends mid-thought | *"But during the year I was working a job with a normal paycheck..."* — trails off | Flop 5 |

When a trigger fires, identify the failure mode before any rewrite:

| Failure Mode | Which Triggers | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Premise Failure** | D9, D11, D16, D17, D18 | **Return to Stage 0.** Choose a different topic. The script cannot be saved. |
| **Delivery Failure** | D1–D8, D10, D12–D15, D19–D22 | **Return to Beat Sheet.** Keep the topic; restructure the script entirely. |

## STAGE 2 — MUST-PASS BINARY CRITERIA

> **Rule:** ALL 20 checks must be **YES**. One **NO** = return to the beat map. Do not proceed to scoring.
>
> **Justification Rule (from File 3):** Every YES must be accompanied by a written justification of no more than 15 words. A "yes" with no justification is recorded as a NO.

### OPENING (First 60 Seconds)

| # | Binary Question | Pass Condition | Outlier Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1** | Does the script open inside a physical space at a specific time, with the viewer's body present, within the first 3 sentences? | Time + place + body — all three | *"It's 7:45 in the morning. You pull into the parking lot."* (Outlier 3) |
| **B2** | Does the first 10 seconds produce a named emotion — dread, envy, recognition, voyeuristic tension, or self-identification — not just "curiosity"? | Name the emotion and quote the line | *"At 4:17 on a Saturday afternoon with a lottery ticket worth $212 million in your pocket..."* (Outlier 6 — voyeuristic tension) |
| **B3** | Is there a recognition sentence ("the way you always do" line) inside the first 120 words? | A private-habit detail the viewer actually does | *"You turn off the engine and you sit there for a second, the way you always do."* (Outlier 3) |
| **B4** | Is the cognitive contract stated plainly within the first 60 seconds — could a viewer repeat it back in one sentence? | Write the contract sentence | *"And that's exactly where the story starts."* (Outlier 3) |
| **B5** | Does the viewer get a specific identity (age, city, income, object) inside the first 200 words? | Concrete details, not generalities | *"22... $2,130 a month."* (Outlier 3) · *"The library on North Michigan opens at 10."* (Outlier 4) |

### PERSPECTIVE & POV

| # | Binary Question | Pass Condition | Outlier Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B6** | Is the script second person present tense for ≥85% of the runtime, with zero first-person personal history? | Count "I/my/me/we" outside attributed dialogue = 0 | Both outliers hold "you" continuously; Flop 4's *"I was there blowing €300,500"* is the failure case |
| **B7** | Does every third-person shift serve the "you" (validate, expand, or raise stakes) and return within 1–2 sentences? | Short shifts only, always returning | Outlier 1: *"60% of Americans are living inside it right now"* — expands then snaps back to "you" |

### NUMBERS & DATA

| # | Binary Question | Pass Condition | Outlier Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B8** | Is every financial figure welded to a body, a moment, or a social consequence — with zero category lists? | 100% anchored; no figure stands alone | *"$92 11 days until payday. You didn't panic. You just got very still."* (Outlier 3) vs. *"Bars and beers, 150 a month"* (Flop 4 — FAIL) |
| **B9** | Is money converted into a non-monetary unit at least once (time, silence, distance, a phone call)? | At least one unit conversion | *"Not $389 a month, $80,000 of future time."* (Outlier 3) |

### STRUCTURE & PROGRESSION

| # | Binary Question | Pass Condition | Outlier Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B10** | Does the script contain a countable ladder or progression with clearly marked staircase units? | Ages, levels, thresholds — viewer always knows their position | Outlier 3: ages 22→42 · Outlier 4: levels 1→8 |
| **B11** | Does the script clearly articulate a "system" or "mechanism" that explains the viewer's financial reality? | A named system, not a behaviour or tip | *"You are not behind. You are exactly where the system placed you."* (Outlier 1) |
| **B12** | Are all transitions narrative-driven with zero explicit structural announcements ("next," "step one," "point two")? | Zero hits on structural markers | Outliers use consequence chains, time jumps, and named prices — never topic labels |
| **B13** | Is the hook under the calculated word cap? | Count it. Cap = `Hook seconds × WPM ÷ 60` | Per script-system L5 |

### TENSION & PACING

| # | Binary Question | Pass Condition | Outlier Evidence |

| **B15** | Does the script name a cost that is NOT denominated in dollars and let it escalate? | Conversation → money → relationship (or equivalent) | *"Here's the first thing stealth wealth actually costs you. Not money. Conversation."* (Outlier 3) |

### NAMED CHARACTERS & DIALOGUE

| # | Binary Question | Pass Condition | Outlier Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B16** | Are there at least three named human characters in the script? | Named, not generic | Outlier 3: Dale, Ree, Jade, Carver, Brida, Ren |

## STAGE 3 — SCORED AUDIT (100 POINTS)

> **Prerequisite:** Only run this after Stages 1 and 2 are completely clean.
>
> **Scoring Scale:**
> - `0` = absent
> - `1` = attempted but broken
> - `2` = present but weak
> - `3` = present and working
> - `4` = present, working, and quotable
>
> **Evidentiary Rule:** No quote from the script in support of a score = automatic 0 for that criterion, regardless of quality.

### A. OPENING — 25 points

| Code | Criterion | Max | What Earns Full Marks |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| A1 | First sentence contains a concrete, sensory, second-person scenario (real object + specific number + time pressure) | **6** | All three in sentence one; specific time + specific space + viewer's body |
| A2 | Named emotion evoked within 10 seconds (dread, envy, recognition, voyeuristic tension, self-identification — NOT "curiosity") | **5** | Emotion is immediate and unmistakable; recognition sentence fires |
| A3 | Cognitive contract unambiguous within first 60 seconds | **5** | Viewer knows exactly what resolution/revelation is promised |
| A4 | Nothing abstract appears before stakes are concrete (no rules, concepts, or definitions pre-stakes) | **5** | First abstraction arrives only after viewer has something to lose |
| A5 | Opening image is specific enough to be false for some viewers and unbearably true for the right ones | **4** | Has an audience, not a general one |

### B. SYSTEM REVELATION & NUMBERS — 20 points

| Code | Criterion | Max | What Earns Full Marks |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| B1 | The system/mechanism is named, not gestured at | **4** | A specific, identifiable system the viewer didn't know had a name |
| B2 | One counter-intuitive claim the viewer suspected but couldn't articulate | **3** | *"What you always read as charisma was actually just structural security"* |
| B3 | Every number welded to body/moment/consequence; at least one unit conversion; zero category lists | **7** | Three-part weld: body + consequence + unit conversion |
| B4 | Numbers function as milestones in the viewer's journey, not exhibits | **4** | Each number marks a position on the staircase |
| B5 | At least one number reveals a mechanism the viewer didn't understand before | **2** | Number as revelation, not decoration |

### C. PERSPECTIVE DISCIPLINE — 10 points

| Code | Criterion | Max | What Earns Full Marks |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| C1 | "You" is the protagonist of every beat | **4** | No section where the viewer is a spectator |
| C2 | Third-person shifts are short, return to "you" within 1–2 sentences, and serve the viewer | **3** | Validation or scale moves only |
| C3 | Zero creator biography, opinion, or experience anywhere | **2** | Not a single "I" of personal history |
| C4 | Narrator has one thing only they could say (wrong opinion, uncertainty, digression) | **1** | Per L17 — breaks AI monotone |

### D. TENSION ARCHITECTURE — 20 points

| Code | Criterion | Max | What Earns Full Marks |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| D1 | Core conflict established inside Beat 1 | **4** | Viewer is in trouble within the first 60 seconds |
| D2 | At least one external obstacle AND one internal doubt before the midpoint | **4** | Both types of pressure present |
| D3 | Beat 6 introduces a new species of problem — one that could NOT have appeared in Beat 1 | **6** | Social cost, isolation, moral weight — not just "same problem bigger" |
| D4 | Minimum two refresh valves used (from Appendix D menu), both evidenced | **4** | Cost + Scale, or Witness + Doubt, etc. |
| D5 | No tension plateau longer than ~2 minutes without a new concrete detail | **2** | Every 90–180 seconds has a scene change, name, or number |

### E. SOCIAL ESCALATION & CRAFT — 15 points

| Code | Criterion | Max | What Earns Full Marks |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| E1 | At least three named characters, each creating a new cost | **5** | Different misreaders at different staircase levels |
| E2 | At least three rule statements / doctrine beats converting scene into law | **4** | *"The second rule, avoidance at this level is not weakness. It is triage."* |
| E3 | Non-monetary cost delivered and escalated: Conversation → Money → Relationship | **4** | Full escalation arc, not just one type of cost |
| E4 | At least two sections end mid-thought (not on a tidy epigram) | **2** | Per L13 — breaks AI voice signature |

### F. ENDING & PAYOFF — 10 points

| Code | Criterion | Max | What Earns Full Marks |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| F1 | Opening image returns verbatim or near-verbatim in the final 60 seconds | **3** | Exact callback with transformed meaning |
| F2 | One elevated, quotable, universal insight (the reframe) | **3** | *"That distinction between having to stay and choosing to stay is the whole story."* |
| F3 | Hands agency to the viewer — a choice, a map, a question for THEIR life | **3** | Ends on the viewer's move, not ours |
| F4 | No summary, no CTA, no moral, last line under 12 words | **1** | Clean exit |

### TOTAL: 100 POINTS

### Score Bands

| Score | Verdict | Action |
|:---:|---|---|
| **0** | AUTOMATIC REJECT | An instant-fail trigger fired. Rewrite from the premise. |
| **1–59** | REJECT | Structural failure. Return to beat map and rewrite failing beats. |
| **60–74** | REWRITE | Spine exists but doesn't hold. Identify two lowest-scoring sections; rewrite those in full. Re-gate. |
| **75–84** | CONDITIONAL PASS | Fix every criterion scored ≤2, then re-gate affected sections. |
| **85–100** | VOICEOVER-READY | Only permitted when Stages 1 and 2 are both completely clean. |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Automatic Rewrite Triggers (regardless of total score):**
> 1. Any single criterion scoring 0
> 2. Any stretch over 60 seconds with no scene change, name, or number
> 3. Any figure that appears once and never returns
> 4. Total below 85 → fix the two lowest line items only, then re-gate

### Stage 3b — DENSITY AUDIT (Binary Pass/Fail Thresholds)

Run these counts AFTER the scored audit. These are not scored — they are binary. One failure = return to draft.

| Metric | How to Count | Threshold | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time to first named emotion | Seconds from cold open | **≤ 10 seconds** | Outliers deliver in the first sentence |
| Time to cognitive contract | Seconds until promise is stated | **≤ 60 seconds** | The autopsy window — viewer decides to stay or leave |
| Creator-"I" narrative sentences | Count every first-person sentence outside dialogue | **0** | Even one is the defect that killed Flop 5 |
| Orphan numbers | Numbers with no consequence/sensation/timestamp in same beat | **0** | The Flop 6 drag mechanism |
| Announced transitions | Instances of "Level X," "next," signposting | **0** | The listicle tell |
| Should-feel statements | Sentences instructing viewer's emotion | **0** | Telling instead of showing |
| Transferable lines | Sentences surviving substitution test | **0** | Self-help tell |
| Stake refreshes | New threats, deadlines, costs, or social confrontations | **≥ 2** | Prevents tension plateau |
| Personal consequences per 3 minutes | Costs/liabilities/exposures landing on "you" | **≥ 1** | Sustains personal stake |
| Return of opening image | Beat 1 image recurs, transformed, at end | **Yes** | Both outliers close the loop; both flops do not |

> **Zero-Tolerance Rule:** *"The zeros are not aspirational targets. They are pass/fail. A single creator-'I' narrative sentence is the exact defect that killed Flop 5; a single announced transition is the exact defect that killed Flop 6."*

### Score Gate Calibration Protocol

> [!TIP]
> **"A gate you haven't calibrated is an opinion."** Before using this gate on a new script, calibrate it:
>
> 1. **Score the reference outlier transcript** (e.g., 929K views) against this rubric. Award originality in full. **Must score ≥ 7.5 out of 10 (i.e., ≥75/100).**
> 2. **Score a flop transcript** from the same niche, similar-sized channel, with under 1/10th of channel average views. **Must score ≤ 5.0 (i.e., ≤50/100).**
> 3. **The separation gate:** Winner and flop must be separated by **≥ 2.5 points (/10)**. If not, the rubric is measuring subjective taste, not retention.
> 4. **Flattery guard:** If the winner scores 9+ and the flop scores 8, the model is flattering. Start a fresh conversation and inject: *"You are scoring generously. Most scripts are a 6. Award only points you can quote evidence for."*

## STAGE 4 — ADVERSARIAL RE-READ

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why this stage exists:** The scored audit (Stage 3) rewards the writer — it measures what the script *does well*. But familiarity breeds blindness. Stage 4 catches what the writer can no longer see by forcing the script through hostile, unfamiliar eyes. Every pass below simulates a specific failure mode that self-assessment cannot detect.

> [!WARNING]
> **Mandatory rules before you begin:**
> - Never audit a script in the same conversation or session that wrote it.
> - No fixes on the gate sheet. When you find a failure, mark it and stop scoring. Return to the draft.
> - One reviewer, one verdict. The gate is not a committee.

Run the following sub-stages **in sequence** — 4A → 4B → 4C → 4D. Do not skip ahead.

---

### 4A — The 12-Hour Cold-Read Rule

**Why this exists:** Fresh eyes are not a metaphor. Cognitive fatigue from writing makes you read *what you meant* instead of *what you wrote*. A 12-hour gap forces your short-term memory of intent to decay, so you encounter the script as a viewer would — cold.

**Protocol:**

1. **Wait.** At least 12 hours must have passed since the draft was completed. No exceptions.
2. **Read aloud.** Read the entire script at conversational pace with a timer running. Silent reading hides rhythm fatigue — your brain auto-corrects cadence problems that a voice will expose.
3. **Record the time.** The sign-off form requires:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| `TIME SINCE DRAFT COMPLETED` | Must be ≥ 12 hours |
| `READ METHOD` | Aloud, timed |

If the 12-hour minimum is not met, **STOP**. You cannot proceed to 4B.

---

### 4B — The 4 Adversarial Passes

Each pass targets a different structural vulnerability. Run them in order.

#### Pass 1 — The 60-Second Stranger Pass

**Why this exists:** A viewer decides whether to stay or leave in the first 60 seconds. This pass tests whether those seconds do what you *think* they do — set up the cognitive contract (the implicit promise of what the video will reveal).

- **Protocol:** Read only **0:00 – 1:00** of the script.
- **Action:** Write down, in one sentence, what you now believe the video will reveal.
- **Evaluation:** Compare your sentence to the intended cognitive contract.
  - If they differ in **any** way → **Beat 1 or Beat 2 is broken.** Return to beat sheet.

#### Pass 2 — The Skip-In Pass

**Why this exists:** Many mid-script sections *feel* important during writing but carry zero narrative weight — they're decoration. This pass exposes them by stripping away context.

- **Protocol:** Read **6:00 – 8:00** in complete isolation, as if you just arrived at that timestamp with no prior context.
- **Evaluation:** Two questions:
  1. Can you tell what the viewer has already been through?
  2. Does the breakthrough in this section mean anything without what came before?
- If the scene **works as a standalone** → it is **not carrying narrative weight**. It is decoration. Mark it.

#### Pass 3 — The Flop Test

**Why this exists:** Writers fall in love with "good writing" — polished paragraphs that sound impressive but are generic enough to appear in any video. This pass kills darlings.

- **Protocol:** Identify the single best paragraph in the script.
- **Evaluation:** Ask: *Could this paragraph have appeared, unchanged, in a flop?* Could it exist in a vlog, a podcast, or a mediocre video essay?
  - If **yes** → cut it or rewrite it.
- **Rule:** Every paragraph in the finished script must be *impossible to imagine* in a vlog or podcast. It must be structurally dependent on this specific narrative.

#### Pass 4 — The Cold-Read Bookend Test

**Why this exists:** Great scripts are emotional loops — the ending should feel like the opening transformed, not like a different video. This pass checks whether the script's emotional arc actually closes.

- **Protocol:** Read only the **first 200 words** and the **last 200 words**, in order, with nothing in between.
- **Pass Condition:** The opening emotion and the closing image are the **same emotional frame, inverted**.
  - Example: If the opening plants dread, the closing should land on earned relief (or dread confirmed — but *transformed* by what the viewer now knows).
  - If they feel disconnected or the closing simply summarizes → **FAIL**.

---

### 4C — The 5-Passage Hostile Read

**Why this exists:** The 4 passes above test specific structural seams. This pass stress-tests the *body* of the script by sampling it randomly, the way a distracted viewer skips around. If 5 random samples hold up under interrogation, the draft is tight. If they don't, the problems are systemic.

**Protocol:** Select **five passages at random** — one from each fifth of the script (roughly: 0–20%, 20–40%, 40–60%, 60–80%, 80–100%). For each passage, ask these 5 questions:

| # | Question | What a marking means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Whose scene is this?** | If the answer is not "the viewer's," mark it. The viewer is always the protagonist. |
| 2 | **What is at risk in this passage, and for whom?** | If nothing is at risk, mark it. Every passage must carry stakes. |
| 3 | **Is every number attached to something human?** | If any number floats without a human anchor (a name, a consequence, a feeling), mark it. |
| 4 | **Could this paragraph appear, unchanged, on a channel in another niche?** | If yes, mark it. The passage is generic. |
| 5 | **Does this passage advance the mechanism, escalate the stakes, or neither?** | If neither, mark it as dead weight. |

---

### 4D — Verdict Rules

Count the **total markings** from sub-stage 4C (the 5-Passage Hostile Read):

| Markings (across all 5 passages) | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | ✅ **PASS (clean)** | Proceed to Stage 5. |
| **1 – 2** | 🔁 **RETURN FOR LINE EDIT** | Fix the marked passages. Gate re-runs from **Stage 2**. |
| **3 or more** | ❌ **REJECT (systemic draft failure)** | The draft has structural rot. Return to the **beat sheet** and rebuild. |

> [!CAUTION]
> A verdict of REJECT means the problems are not local — they are baked into the draft's DNA. Line edits will not save it. Start over from the beat sheet.

### 5B — Banned Structural Patterns

Each pattern below describes a *type* of writing that is prohibited, regardless of the specific words used. The reviewer must read for these structures, not just Ctrl+F for phrases.

| # | Pattern | What It Looks Like | Why It's Banned |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opening on a negation of an unintroduced thing** | "It's not about the money." (before the viewer knows what *it* is) | Confuses the viewer before the contract is set. |
| 2 | **Instructing the viewer to build a scene** | "Picture this: you're at a coffee shop…" | Breaks immersion. The script builds the scene *for* them. |
| 3 | **First-person personal history anywhere** | "When I was 22, I made this mistake…" | The viewer is the protagonist, not the narrator. |
| 4 | **Announced step structure** | "Step one: track your spending. Step two: cut subscriptions." | Listicle format. Kills narrative momentum. |
| 5 | **Reading a category list with figures** | "Housing: 35%. Food: 15%. Transport: 12%..." | Unanchored data dump. Numbers must attach to something human. |
| 6 | **Imperative instruction mid-story** | "Now open your banking app and check your balance." | Breaks the narrative to lecture. |
| 7 | **Stating or implying nothing is at stake** | "This isn't a big deal, but…" / "It doesn't really matter…" | If nothing is at stake, the scene has no reason to exist. |
| 8 | **Admitting the video has no resolution** | "There's no easy answer…" / "It depends on your situation." | The script must resolve. Ambiguity is earned, not surrendered. |
| 9 | **Disclaimer cascade that denies the premise** | "This isn't financial advice. Everyone's situation is different. Consult a professional." | Undermines the entire video's authority in one paragraph. |
| 10 | **Unreconciled opening figures** | Dropping a shocking number in the opening and never returning to it. | Broken promise. Every number introduced must pay off. |
| 11 | **CTA stack** | "Like, subscribe, hit the bell, comment, share, join our Patreon." | Multiple CTAs dilute each other and break the closing. |
| 12 | **Mood statements in place of claims** | "It feels like the economy is struggling." | A mood is not a claim. Make a falsifiable assertion. |
| 13 | **Any sentence surviving the substitution test** | If you can swap in a different topic and the sentence still works, it's generic. | Proves the sentence carries no specific narrative weight. |
| 14 | **Act-percentage scaffolding** | "Act 1 (0–25%), Act 2 (25–75%), Act 3 (75–100%)" visible in the script | Structural scaffolding is for outlines, not finished scripts. |
| 15 | **More than 60 seconds without a scene change, name, or number** | A long stretch of abstract narration with no concrete anchors. | The viewer's attention drifts without sensory hooks. |
| 16 | **Didactic lecturing before context** | Naming a rule or principle before showing its effect on a person. | Inverts the correct order: consequence → then name. |
| 17 | **Narrator first-person intrusion** | "I," "my," "me," "we," "our," "us" outside of quoted dialogue. | The narrator is invisible. The viewer is the only "you." |
| 18 | **Abstract philosophical discussion before stakes** | "Hope deferred…" / "Consumerism purgatory…" before anyone is hurt. | Philosophy without stakes is a lecture. |
| 19 | **Ungrounded conceptual jargon** | Naming a concept without an immediate second-person example. | Jargon without grounding is noise. |
| 20 | **Generic self-help language and platitudes** | "Believe in yourself." / "Start your journey today." | Empty calories. Says nothing specific. |
| 21 | **Interview/conversation structure surviving into script** | Q&A format, "So tell me about…", dialogue markers from research interviews. | The script is a narrative, not a transcript. |
| 22 | **Listicle/wealth-ladder/tier taxonomies** | "Tier 1: Broke. Tier 2: Comfortable. Tier 3: Wealthy." | Taxonomies are not stories. |
| 23 | **Announced transitions** | "Now let's move on to…" / "Speaking of which…" | If you have to announce the transition, it isn't one. |
| 24 | **Sterile percentage delivery** | Percentages stated without human context or consequence. | Numbers must bleed. |
| 25 | **Telling the viewer what to feel** | "This is heartbreaking." / "You should be angry about this." | Show, don't tell. The emotion must arise from the scene. |
| 26 | **Definitions before consequences** | "Compound interest is when…" before showing what it *does* to someone. | Consequence first, definition later (if ever). |
| 27 | **System-perspective over protagonist-perspective** | "The economy works by…" instead of "Your paycheck shrinks because…" | The viewer lives inside the system, not above it. |
| 28 | **Endings that summarise rather than resolve** | "So today we learned…" / "To recap the three points…" | A summary is not a resolution. The ending must *land*, not review. |
| 29 | **Endings that stop mid-thought** | Script just… stops. No closing image, no emotional landing. | The viewer must feel the loop close. |

### 5C — AI-Voice Tells

These patterns are the specific fingerprints of machine-generated or machine-assisted writing. They make scripts sound artificial even when the content is correct. Each has a hard cap or rule.

| # | Tell | Rule | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Universal epigrams ending every section** | At least **2 sections** must end mid-thought, not with a neat bow. | Real narrators don't gift-wrap every paragraph. Epigram-capping is the #1 tell of AI prose. |
| 2 | **Antithesis capping** ("Not X. Y." / "Gross. Effective.") | Must occur **≤ 8 times** in the entire script. | Antithesis is a powerful tool — until it appears every 90 seconds and becomes a tic. |
| 3 | **Consecutive structural repetition** | No sentence structure may repeat **3 times consecutively**. | Three identical cadences in a row create a robotic rhythm the ear rejects. |
| 4 | **"Here's the thing" / "Here's what's strange" openers** | **Strictly limited** — treat as near-banned. One occurrence maximum, and only if it is structurally earned. | These are crutch openers. They signal the writer couldn't find a real transition. |
| 5 | **Honesty disclaimers** | **No more than ONE** per script. ("To be honest…" / "The truth is…" / "I'll be real with you…") | Multiple disclaimers imply everything else is dishonest. One is a beat; two is a pattern. |
| 6 | **Missing narrator signature** | The script **must** include at least one: a wrong opinion, a moment of genuine uncertainty, or a digression that doesn't serve the argument. | A script with zero imperfections is a script with zero personality. The narrator must feel human. Absence of a signature = **automatic fail**. |

> [!CAUTION]
> **Enforcement:** A single hit from 5A or 5B sends the script back to **Stage 1** for a full gate re-run. AI-Voice Tells (5C) that exceed their caps are treated identically — full gate re-run from Stage 1. There are no partial passes.

## STAGE 6 — PRODUCTION PASS

> **Why this stage exists:** Stages 1–5 ensure the script is *good*. Stage 6 ensures it's *producible*. A brilliant script that reaches voiceover without phonetic spellings, visual plans, or verified facts will produce a bad video. These four checks close the gap between script and production.

### 6A — TTS PREPARATION

Before handoff to ElevenLabs, produce a **second, clean copy** of the script dedicated to text-to-speech. This copy must satisfy all of the following:

| # | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Visual tags removed** — All `[V: ...]` tags stripped completely | The TTS copy contains zero square-bracket visual tags |
| 2 | **Phonetic spelling** — Unfamiliar names spelled phonetically on first use | `Mezhyrich [MEZH-ih-rich]` · Keep a pronunciation list at the end |
| 3 | **Spoken numbers** — All numbers written as spoken words | "eighteen thousand years ago" not "18,250 BP" · "$four thousand two hundred" not "$4,200" |
| 4 | **Dates, units, currencies expanded** | "dollars" not "$" · "percent" not "%" · "two thousand twenty-five" not "2025" |
| 5 | **Breathing & pauses** — Paragraph breaks where breaths belong | Break at natural clause boundaries, not mid-thought |
| 6 | **No parenthetical asides** — Rewrite as separate sentences | AI voice flattens parentheticals — they lose meaning |
| 7 | **Breath-length splitting** — Read every sentence aloud; split any the voice can't land in one breath | Long compound sentences must be broken |

**TTS Copy Report (must accompany the handoff):**
- [ ] Total words
- [ ] Runtime at measured WPM
- [ ] Honesty disclaimers used (max 1)
- [ ] Full `[CONTESTED]` claims list
- [ ] Pronunciation list

### 6B — VISUAL TAGS

Every paragraph in the main script (not the TTS copy) must specify what appears on screen:

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Tag format** | `[V: archival photo of the stealth wealth parking lot scene]` |
| **Missing visual** | Any line with no possible visual gets: `[V: NONE — rewrite or cut]` |
| **Zero [V: NONE] gate** | Before handoff, ALL `[V: NONE]` lines must be rewritten or cut. Zero tolerance. |
| **Reporting** | Report total count of `[V: NONE]` tags in the gate form |
| **Clean TTS** | All visual tags stripped from the TTS copy |

### 6C — FACT-CHECK PROTOCOL

> [!WARNING]
> **AI models invent citations with plausible journal names, real authors, and fake DOIs.** Never trust a citation you haven't opened.

**For every source cited in the script, 6 elements are mandatory:**

| # | Required Element |
|---|---|
| 1 | Exact title |
| 2 | Author(s) |
| 3 | Year |
| 4 | Publication or institution |
| 5 | An openable URL or DOI |
| 6 | **The exact supporting sentence from that source in quotation marks** |

**No quoted sentence = UNVERIFIED. Guessing or reconstructing citations from memory is banned.**

**Citation Tiers (strongest to weakest):**

| Tier | What Qualifies | Use For |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Primary** | Court filings, SEC filings, patents, government reports, company annual reports, original photographs, original text | Core claims, legal/financial facts |
| **Tier 2 — Peer-Reviewed** | Named journal, named authors, year, DOI (Nature, Science, PNAS, etc.) | Scientific claims, research findings |
| **Tier 3 — Institutional** | Bodies with reputation to lose (NASA, NIST, Smithsonian, NTSB, national archives) | Institutional facts, public data |
| **Tier 4 — Quality Journalism** | Reuters, AP, FT, WSJ, NYT, BBC — only where reporting names its own sources | Business news, recent events ONLY |

**Banned Sources (Zero Exceptions):**
- ❌ Another YouTube video (including the reference transcript — laundering unverified claims)
- ❌ AI output / LLM memory without a citation
- ❌ Content farms, SEO listicles, "10 amazing facts" sites, Medium, Quora, Reddit, Pinterest, TikTok, Facebook
- ❌ Wikipedia as a terminal source (follow footnotes to what it actually cites; dead footnote = UNVERIFIED)

**The 5 Failure Classes (scan every script for these):**

| # | Class | What to Look For |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Derived Precision** | Turning a date range into a single figure or duration. Check every "for X years," average, and percentage. |
| 2 | **Laundered Source** | Cited to a blog/video, then stated with academic authority |
| 3 | **Contested as Settled** | A disputed position presented as established fact — tag `[CONTESTED]` |
| 4 | **Internal Inconsistency** | Same fact given two different values in the script |
| 5 | **Out-of-Scope Evidence** | Evidence from the wrong period, region, or field supporting a claim |

**Spot-Check:** Reviewer opens 3 URLs of the lowest-confidence claims. Dead link or title mismatch = hallucinated citations → throw out the fact-check entirely.

**Pass Threshold:** Confirmed claim ratio below ~70% = script fails (built on assumption, not research).

### 6D — HOOK ROTATION

> **Why this exists:** Templated sameness across videos is a recognized risk under YouTube's **inauthentic-content policy** (mass-produced, repetitious content). This is not an aesthetic preference — it's a policy defense.

**Step 1:** Check which hook types the last 3 published videos used.

**Step 2:** Choose a different hook type from this menu:

| # | Hook Type | What It Does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mystery object** | Opens on a physical thing the viewer can't explain |
| 2 | **Live stake** | Viewer is in danger / losing something right now |
| 3 | **Anomaly** | A number or fact that doesn't fit the pattern |
| 4 | **Scenario** | "You are standing in..." — immersive second-person scene |
| 5 | **Uncomfortable claim** | A statement most viewers believe is wrong (but isn't) |
| 6 | **Reversal** | Opens at the summit then yanks to the floor |

**Step 3:** Write **3 hooks** of different types. Each must:
- Land strictly under the calculated hook cap (words)
- Confirm and pay off the video title's promise
- Be labeled with hook type and word count

**Step 4:** Recommend one with an explicit craft rationale. Wait for confirmation before writing the first half.

**Rule:** Never repeat the same hook type twice in a row across published videos.

## APPENDIX A — THE UNIVERSAL BEAT MAP (Three Versions)

Choose the format that matches your script's topic type. All three are valid; they emphasize different strengths.

### Version 1: The 6-Beat Format (System-Reveal Scripts)

Best for: Mechanism/system-reveal topics — corporate acquisition, banking tiers, tax strategies

| # | Beat | Function | Position | Must Contain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Immediate Visceral Conflict | Make the viewer the subject of a problem | First 60s | Specific time/place; personal cost |
| **2** | How It Works | Supply the mechanism | Early–mid | System explained through "your" actions |
| **3** | Hidden Truth | Reframe what they thought they knew | Mid | Counter-intuitive fact with social consequence |
| **4** | Escalation | Convert understanding into liability | Mid–late | A cost that lands on "you" personally |
| **5** | Turning Point | Let knowledge be applied | Late | A decisive act by "you" |
| **6** | Resolution | Re-read the opening image | End | Beat 1 image, now meaning something else |

### Version 2: The 8-Beat 15-Minute Scaffold (Journey/Transformation Scripts)

Best for: Progression topics — wealth levels, life stages, career transformation

| Beat | Name | Window | Tension State | Must Be On The Page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | The Immediate Visceral Problem | **0:00–1:00** | **Spike** | One "you" scenario, present tense, ≥3 concrete details. Emotion in 10s. Contract implied. |
| **2** | The System / Mechanism Revelation | **1:00–2:30** | **Spike→Drop** | The mechanism that produced Beat 1. One counter-intuitive claim. One "you are not alone" scale moment. |
| **3** | The First Crack / Small Shift | **2:30–4:00** | **Rise** | A shift in *perception*, not a tip. A change in how the viewer sees, producing a change in what they do. |
| **4** | The Unseen Progress | **4:00–6:00** | **Plateau ⚠️ DANGER ZONE 1** | Quiet compounding. ≥2 time-stamped milestones. Reinvestment logic, not willpower. Every 90s needs a concrete detail. |
| **5** | The First Breakthrough | **6:00–8:00** | **Rise→Peak** | External validation: an outside party confirms the change. Dialogue or specific detail. |
| **6** | The Unforeseen Cost | **8:00–10:30** | **Drop→Rise ⚠️ DANGER ZONE 2** | **Refresh valve.** New species of pressure: social cost, isolation, moral weight. Must NOT be Beat 1 at bigger scale. |
| **7** | The Re-framing | **10:30–13:00** | **Rise** | The quotable line. Reframe through a person, moment, or single line of dialogue. |
| **8** | The Empowerment | **13:00–15:00** | **Resolution** | Return to opening image, transformed. Hand agency: a choice, a map, a question for the viewer's life. |

**Tension Curve:**
`SPIKE → drop → rise → PLATEAU (danger) → PEAK → drop → new rise → RESOLVE`

### Version 3: The 11-Beat Format (Stealth-Wealth / Social Escalation Scripts)

Best for: POV topics with named characters — "Your Life If You LOOK POOR," "Every Level of Wealth by Your Sunday"

| # | Beat | Function | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Cold Immersion | Body in a room, recognition fires before any idea does | EARLY — first 3 sentences |
| **2** | The Unspoken Advantage | Status inversion: viewer is misread AND knows more | EARLY — inside 100 words |
| **3** | The Hard Number | One figure welded to body/timestamp/consequence | EARLY-MID — first 90s |
| **4** | The Staircase Unit | Countable progression; viewer always knows position | MID — repeating, 90-180s |
| **5** | The New Misreader | Fresh named character per unit; escalates socially | MID — one per unit |
| **6** | The Rule Statement | Scene converted into universal law; tension re-arms | MID — after each unit |
| **7** | The Unit Conversion | Money reframed as time/silence/distance | MID-LATE — at least once |
| **8** | The Hidden Door | System was watching all along; invisibility paid off | LATE |
| **9** | The Cost That Isn't Money | Price paid in relationships/silence/asymmetry | LATE |
| **10** | The Reframe | Flips viewer's reading in one sentence | LATE — final third |
| **11** | The Loop Close | Opening image returns; one question unresolved | END — final 60s |

## APPENDIX B — NAMED TECHNIQUES GLOSSARY

An alphabetical reference of every named writing technique extracted from the outlier transcripts.

| # | Technique | Definition | Outlier Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Audit as Accusation** | Balance-sheet figures delivered as moral indictment of authority | *"The balance of the reserve fund is $126,000. The fully funded balance is $1,350,000. That's 9.3% funded."* |
| 2 | **Cognitive Contract** | Clear 1-sentence promise of resolution within 60 seconds | *"And that's exactly where the story starts."* |
| 3 | **Contract Resolved in Dialogue** | Snapping tension shut through understated spoken exchange | *"She says, 'I thought you were the one who was struggling.' You say some of us were."* |
| 4 | **Data Embedding** | Numbers anchored to sensation, consequence, or moment — never standalone | *"$92 11 days until payday. You didn't panic. You just got very still."* |
| 5 | **Doctrine Aside** | 2-4 sentence exit explaining mechanism without breaking POV | *"Here's the first thing stealth wealth actually costs you. Not money. Conversation."* |
| 6 | **Ethos Without Moralising** | Channel philosophy through narrative outcome, not sermon | *"None of this ever needed an audience, and the next story won't either."* |
| 7 | **Forward-Planted Promise** | Explicit future confrontation with a date attached | *"14 months from now, this same woman will stand in a packed clubhouse and ask for help."* |
| 8 | **Hidden Door** | Revealing a system secretly observing viewer's discipline | *"That year, a door opens that you didn't know existed... Nobody told you this door existed."* |
| 9 | **Insider Document** | Institutional rules delivered via physical documents arriving in-scene | Outlier 4's weekly briefing; Outlier 3's loan officer's automated preferential rate offer |
| 10 | **Loop Close / Two-Move Close** | Opening image returns verbatim + physical action + unresolved question | *"It is 7:14 on a Sunday morning... You pick up the phone. You put it back down."* |
| 11 | **Mundane-Then-Impossible** | Boring routine detonated by extraordinary financial reality in one clause | *"You badge in. You pour coffee from the same machine... And somewhere inside a filing cabinet, your name is attached to the majority of the equity."* |
| 12 | **New Misreader** | Fresh named character per staircase unit; escalates socially | Act 1: Ree (open house) → Act 2: Jade (torn back seat) → Act 3: Brida (break room) → Act 4: Ren (Tesla) |
| 13 | **Non-Monetary Cost Escalation** | Fixed sequence: Conversation → Money → Relationship | *"the way a friendship bends slightly and never quite returns to where it was"* |
| 14 | **Number + Pre-Emptive Correction** | Large figure with false assumption killed in same breath | *"Your equity value is approximately 780,000. That is not a profit you can extract. It is a paper position."* |
| 15 | **Numeric Juxtaposition** | Two contrasting figures side-by-side, no editorializing | *"Your house is worth $197,000. Hers, three doors down, is worth $718,000."* |
| 16 | **Physical Reversal** | Physical details before corporate/legal abstractions | Anchors to "a filing cabinet," "a registered document" before explaining LLC structure |
| 17 | **Pre-Emptive Denial** | Killing viewer's skepticism before it causes drop-off | *"That is not a crime. It is not a trick. It is how private company ownership has always worked."* |
| 18 | **Recognition Sentence** | Private-habit line in first 120 words proving the video knows the viewer | *"You turn off the engine and you sit there for a second, the way you always do."* |
| 19 | **Reframe** | One sentence flipping viewer's reading, aimed at their own life | *"That distinction between having to stay and choosing to stay is the whole story."* |
| 20 | **Reverse-Order Hook** | Summit-to-floor reset (peak → bottom → climb) | Outlier 4: Opens on 43rd floor with $1.3M portfolio shift, then slams to Level 1 with $31 in pocket |
| 21 | **Rule Statement** | Scene converted into universal psychological law | *"The second rule, avoidance at this level is not weakness. It is triage."* |
| 22 | **Staircase Unit** | Countable repeating progression giving the script a physical spine | Outlier 3: ages 22→42 · Outlier 4: levels 1→8+ |
| 23 | **Strategic Third-Person Shift** | 1-2 sentences out to validate/universalize, then snap back to "you" | *"60% of Americans are living inside it right now without knowing it has a name."* |
| 24 | **Substitution Test** | If a sentence works on a cooking/gaming channel, delete it | Anti-slop test applied to every line |
| 25 | **Three-Part Number Weld** | Figure attached to (1) body/moment + (2) consequence + (3) unit conversion | *$3,800:* attached to handshake + seller disbelief + cash count |
| 26 | **Timestamped Opening** | Specific clock time signaling reported event, not hypothetical | *"At 4:17 on a Saturday afternoon..."* |
| 27 | **Unit Conversion** | Dollar amounts → non-monetary experiential units (time, silence, distance) | *"Not $389 a month, $80,000 of future time."* |
| 28 | **Unspoken Advantage** | Observer misreads viewer, but viewer holds superior hidden information | *"You notice you don't move because you know something she doesn't."* |

## APPENDIX C — APPROVED TRANSITION SHAPES

Every transition must carry **either a new stake OR a forward promise.** Topic labels ("Next," "Step two") are permanently banned.

| # | Shape | How It Works | Outlier Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Assumption Correction** | Corrects viewer's assumption mid-sentence; moves time forward | *"But something shifts at 25, not in the account. In the question."* |
| 2 | **The Named Price** | Sets up new unit by explicitly naming the non-monetary cost | *"Here's the first thing stealth wealth actually costs you. Not money. Conversation."* |
| 3 | **The Hidden Door** | Forward-pull implying a secret system, then unveiling it | *"That year, a door opens that you didn't know existed."* |
| 4 | **The Bare Unit Address** | Advances the ladder with clean, zero-fanfare numbering | *"Level two."* — no preamble, no explanation |
| 5 | **The Time-and-Number Jump** | Advances time AND account simultaneously in one sentence | *"The account crosses $60,000 on a Sunday evening."* |
| 6 | **The Doctrine Close-then-Reopen** | Closes a philosophical beat; pivots into a raw scene | *"This is the part nobody puts in the highlight reel."* |
| 7 | **Transition by Consequence** | Moves to next topic via causal logic | *"Which brings you to the two layers that create the invisibility."* |
| 8 | **Transition by In-Scene Escalation** | Heightens stakes of a boundary within the scene | *"Now, here is the line you do not cross. And this is not a footnote."* |
| 9 | **Transition by Time Jump** | Jumps to new time/setting/event with no verbal announcement | *"One morning in March, at 6:00 a.m., a flatbed backs into the Straoud's driveway."* |

## APPENDIX D — TENSION REFRESH VALVE MENU

When tension needs refreshing, use one of these four. **Minimum 2 per script.**

| Valve | What It Does | When to Use | Outlier Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Progress creates a new, non-financial price | Beat 6 (the unforeseen cost) | *"you wake up some mornings and look at everything you've built and feel something you can't name… something closer to vertigo"* |
| **Scale** | Zoom out to same mechanism operating on someone else | Mid-script validation | *"Right now, a 24 year old somewhere in this country just checked their bank account. $340 until Friday."* |
| **Witness** | An outside party's changed perception of the viewer | After a milestone | *"you watch them recalibrate, a small mental adjustment, downgrading their estimate of you. That downgrade is not an insult. It is protection."* |
| **Doubt** | The viewer's own internal objection, voiced aloud | Beat 4 (quiet compounding) | *"You think, 'Is this stupid? This feels stupid.'"* |

**Mandatory placement:**
- One at **Beat 4** (a small doubt — keeps the viewer engaged during the plateau)
- One at **Beat 6** (the major unforeseen cost — this is the tension re-arm that carries the second half)

## APPENDIX E — DANGER ZONES

Two points in every 15-minute script where writers consistently fail:

### Danger Zone 1: 4:00–6:00 (Beat 4 — Quiet Compounding)

**The risk:** The tension curve intentionally plateaus here to give the viewer a breath. Writers mistake this for permission to lecture — they fill it with advice, definitions, and textbook explanations.

**The rule:** It must be a plateau of **story**, not explanation. Every 90 seconds of this window must contain a **new concrete detail** (a place, a person, a number attached to a moment). Rely on reinvestment logic and lived experience, not willpower or financial definitions.

### Danger Zone 2: 8:00–10:30 (Beat 6 — Unforeseen Cost)

**The risk:** The script risks collapsing back into the opening problem — repeating the struggle of Beat 1, just at a bigger scale.

**The rule:** The cost must be a **new species of problem.** If your Beat 6 could be swapped into Beat 1 without a rewrite, the script has no arc. It must introduce a non-financial pressure that money cannot solve: isolation, social recalibration, moral weight, or vertigo.

## APPENDIX G — OPENING & CLOSING QUICK REFERENCE

### OPENING (First 60 Seconds)

**✅ DO:**
| Timing | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Sentences 1–3 | Physical space + specific time + viewer's body |
| Inside 10 seconds | Named emotion (dread, envy, recognition, voyeuristic tension, self-identification) |
| Inside 100 words | First status friction — someone misreading/judging the viewer |
| Inside 120 words | Recognition sentence ("the way you always do") + cognitive contract |
| Inside 90 seconds | Hard financial number welded to body/timestamp/consequence |
| Inside 90 seconds | Pre-emptive denial (kill viewer's skepticism) |
| Inside 200 words | Viewer's specific identity (age, city, income, object) |

**❌ DON'T:**
- Never open with negation (*"I don't follow those people"*)
- Never open with "Picture this"
- Never open with rule/step/list
- Never open in first person
- Never open with unreconcilable figure
- Never open with autobiography (*"It was in 2018..."*)
- Never open with system explanation before personal stakes

### CLOSING (Final 60 Seconds)

**✅ DO:**
| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Loop close | Opening image returns verbatim or near-verbatim, meaning transformed |
| Physical action | Undramatic action — the viewer doing something small |
| Open question | One human question left unresolved |
| Agency | Hand off to the viewer's life, not the channel |
| Last line | Under 12 words |
| Ethos | Through narrative outcome, not sermon |

**❌ DON'T:**
- No moral, lesson, or summary
- No disclaimer cascade
- No CTA stack (zero: like/subscribe/comment/share)
- No trailing off mid-thought
- No taxonomy summary (*"At the bottom... At the top..."*)

## APPENDIX H — PERSPECTIVE RULES

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Dominant POV** | Second person, present tense for ≥85% of runtime |
| **Narrator biography** | ZERO. The narrator has no personal history. Never report what the writer did, bought, learned, or felt. |
| **"I" tolerance** | Zero outside attributed character dialogue |
| **Permitted register switches** | 3 types only (never person switches): |
| | 1. **Rule statement** — brief drop into 3rd-person aphorism |
| | 2. **Quoted dialogue** — other characters quoted inside the 2nd-person frame |

| **Named characters** | At least 3 per script, with proper names (Dale, Ree, Jade — not "a friend" or "someone") |
| **Dialogue per act** | Every act must contain at least one quoted human voice |

## APPENDIX J — TITLE-TO-SCRIPT ALIGNMENT TEST

**Score every script on a 1–10 scale using these 4 diagnostic questions:**

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| 1 | What did the title promise the viewer would *experience*? |
| 2 | Did the script deliver on that promise within the first 60 seconds? |
| 3 | Did the script fully resolve the title's implied question by the end? |
| 4 | Where, specifically, did any misalignment occur? |

**Score Bands:**

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **9–10** | Promise delivered in opening and resolved by end |
| **6–8** | Promise delivered late, or resolved only partially |
| **3–5** | Promise implied but displaced by a narrower subject |
| **1–2** | Promise contradicted by script's actual format |

**Ship Threshold:** Scripts scoring **< 6** do not ship — either the title is rewritten to match the script, or the script is rewritten to honour the title.

> [!WARNING]
> **The Flop 6 Paradox:** Flop 6 scored **9/10 on alignment** and still died. Title alignment is necessary and **nowhere near sufficient.** A perfectly aligned script can die of delivery failure. This is why all 7 stages exist — alignment is just one check.

## APPENDIX K — THE FLOP 6 CASE STUDY (Why Alignment Alone Fails)

**This is the single most important lesson in the entire gate.**

- **Title:** "How Banks Treat You at Different Wealth Levels"
- **Alignment Score:** 9/10 (delivered in 60s, systematically covered all tiers, resolved bottom-to-top)
- **Result:** Flopped.

**Why it died despite near-perfect alignment:**

| # | Failure | What Happened |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Announced listicle frame | Built around headers ("Level one... Level two... Level eight"), forcing the viewer to be re-seated 8 times |
| 2 | Abstraction before stakes | System mechanics explained before personal danger |
| 3 | Telling vs. showing | *"This is what financial stress actually feels like"* — instructed emotion instead of creating it |
| 4 | Sterile numbers | Percentages (0.01%, 23% APR) as factual entries, not personal liabilities |
| 5 | No sustained stakes | After the gas-station scene, it became an observational essay |

**The law this teaches:** A script can promise exactly the right thing and deliver exactly the right information — and still fail because it *lectures* instead of *immersing*. The topic was strong (Delivery Failure). Only the structure killed it.

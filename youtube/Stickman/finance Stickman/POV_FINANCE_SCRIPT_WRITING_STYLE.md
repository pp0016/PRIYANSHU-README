## SECTION 1 — THE CORE IDENTITY

### 1.1 What This Format IS

A second-person present-tense financial coming-of-age story. The viewer IS the protagonist. They start with nothing. They make one small decision. Over 16–20 years, through raises, crashes, relationships, and temptations, that decision compounds into seven figures. Nobody around them ever knows.

**The thesis in one line:** Invisibility is the strategy, and invisibility is the cost.

### 1.2 Non-Negotiable Constants (Present in 14/14 Scripts)

| # | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Timestamped cold open** | "It's 12:14 on a Tuesday." / "It's 3:47 on a Tuesday afternoon." |
| 2 | **Mundane physical action in a specific place** | "You're sitting at the far table in the breakroom, the one under the flickering fluorescent tube nobody's bothered to fix." |
| 3 | **One sensory detail** | The flickering fluorescent, the burnt coffee, the 98° thermometer, the hum of a vending machine |
| 4 | **A named foil called Daniel** | Always the spender. Never written as stupid. His collapse arrives as one factual line without commentary |
| 5 | **"Level one / two / three…" chapter cards** | Always spoken aloud. Always named evocatively: "Level two, the boring years." |
| 6 | **A balance figure spoken aloud at each level boundary** | "$6,940" / "$43,600" / "$263,800" / "$1,046,700" |
| 7 | **The reinvestment rule** | When a raise lands, the contribution rises before the new money reaches checking |
| 8 | **Locked second-person present tense in every scene** | "You don't sell. You don't even seriously consider it." |
| 9 | **The crossover beat** | Portfolio growth meets or exceeds earned income — two numbers placed side by side |
| 10 | **Coda reprise of the opening scene, same timestamp** | "It's 12:14 on a Tuesday. Same table, same flickering tube." |
| 11 | **One recurring physical object** | The scratched Tupperware, the thermos, the yellow legal pad, the silver house key, the dented toolbox |
| 12 | **A mentor figure with one aphorism decoded later** | Christopher: "You won't feel rich doing this. That's how you know it's working." / Daniel: "First, you stop the leak, then you dry the wall, then you paint." |
| 13 | **Three-part CTA + fixed sign-off** | Conditional callback → like → comment question → subscribe → "Thanks for spending this time with me. I'll see you in the next one." |
| 14 | **Invisibility as the thesis** | "What changed is invisible by design." |

### 1.3 Voice Rules (Absolute)

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **POV** | Second person present tense, locked. No exceptions inside scenes |
| **Past tense** | Only for the rewind body ("You bumped your 401k to 8%"), present for frame scenes |
| **Third person** | Only for foils and mentors — Daniel, Christopher, Ashley, Jessica |
| **First person** | Reserved exclusively for the CTA — "I'll see you in the next one"; "I read those" |
| **Dialogue economy** | 1–3 quoted lines per script, always aphoristic. Never conversational back-and-forth |
| **Register shift** | Essayistic tone only at mechanism sections — the only place the narration addresses listener scepticism directly |

### 1.4 Anti-Patterns — What POV Finance NEVER Does (Verified 0/14)

1. **No teaching voice** — the script never instructs the viewer ("you should…", "make sure to…")
2. **No first-person authority** — the creator never says "I did this" or "in my experience"
3. **No product, ticker, platform or fund names** — no "Vanguard," no "S&P 500," no "Fidelity"
4. **No lifestyle payoff** — nobody upgrades the house, the car or the watch at the end. The Camry, Tacoma, Corolla, Elantra persist through every ending
5. **No gloating over the foil** — Daniel is never written as stupid. His collapse is stated as one factual line
6. **No "hey guys, welcome back"** — no ad-lib, no banter, no outtakes
7. **No politics, no news** beyond ambient market weather ("the market drops 14%")
8. **No profanity, no meme language, no slang**
9. **No numbered tips, listicles, or announced roadmaps**
10. **No mid-video CTA** — the only CTA is terminal, after the coda reprise

### 2.2 Level Count

- **Mode:** 6 levels (most popular-era scripts)
- **Range:** 5–7 levels
- **Latest era:** Often 7 levels, sometimes with sub-levels
- Every level MUST have:
  1. A setup (scene, time jump, new situation)
  2. A money friction (temptation, crash, expense, foil comparison)
  3. One decision the viewer makes
  4. A spoken balance figure
  5. A foil contrast (what Daniel / Christopher / the outside world does differently)

## SECTION 3 — THE HOOK PLAYBOOK

### 3.1 Hook Construction (Exact Formula — 0% to 5% of Runtime)

The hook is always built in this exact sequence:

**Step 1: Timestamped Sensory Scenario Drop**
```
"It's [HH:MM] on a [DAY]. You're [MUNDANE ACTION] in [SPECIFIC PLACE],
[ONE SENSORY DETAIL: flickering tube / burnt coffee / 98° thermometer /
hum of a vending machine / key on a keyring]."
```

Verbatim examples from transcripts:
- "It's 12:14 on a Tuesday. You're sitting at the far table in the breakroom, the one under the flickering fluorescent tube nobody's bothered to fix."
- "It's 3:47 on a Tuesday afternoon. You're sitting in a 2012 Toyota Tacoma in the parking lot of the county courthouse. The engine is off."

**Step 2: Foil Object, Priced**
A visible object belonging to someone else — Daniel's truck, the phone showing financing terms, the $187 dinner check.

**Step 3: Number-Drop Reveal + Invisibility Line**
```
"Your [ACCOUNT] closed [DAY] at [$FIGURE]. Nobody in this [ROOM] knows that.
Not [FOIL]. Not [SECOND NAME]. Not [THIRD NAME]."
```

Verbatim:
- "The number was $1,046,700. Nobody in this break room knows that. Not Daniel. Not the new guy still learning the scanner gun. Not your shift manager who signs off on your time card every two weeks like you're anyone else on the floor."
- "By then, your accounts will hold more than three times what the two of you built together. Nobody in that house will know."

**Step 4: Negative-Knowledge Signpost**
```
"Here's the strange part." / "That's the part that still catches you sometimes."
```

**Step 5: Thesis Negation (Used in 10/14 Scripts)**
```
"This isn't a story about [BEING SMARTER/FRUGAL/LUCKY].
It's a story about [WHAT HAPPENED WHEN YOU STOPPED NEEDING PEOPLE TO KNOW]."
```

**Step 6: Rewind**
```
"[N] years ago, you started here for $[WAGE] an hour.
Nothing about that story explains what happened since. So, here's what does."
```

### 3.2 Hook Variants (Ranked by Frequency)

| Rank | Type | Count | When to Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Timestamped sensory scenario | 14/14 | ALWAYS — this is the foundation, combined with all below |
| 2 | Number-drop reveal + invisibility | 14/14 | ALWAYS — the balance is always in the hook |
| 3 | Foil-object contrast | 14/14 | ALWAYS — Daniel's truck, someone's status purchase |
| 4 | Negative-knowledge signpost | 14/14 | ALWAYS — "here's the strange part" |
| 5 | Thesis negation | 10/14 | Default. Skip only when the opening scenario IS the thesis |

| 7 | Scene-into-question | 3/14 | Strongest variant — converts the cold open into an explicit question |
| 8 | Flash-forward promise | 1/14 | Used in divorce rebuild script — "15 years from now, you'll be back in that same kitchen" |

## SECTION 4 — TENSION ARCHITECTURE

### 4.1 The Six Signature Tension Devices

**Device A: The Number-in-the-Pocket Loop**
The balance is stated in the hook and withheld from every other character for the full runtime. This is the PRIMARY tension mechanism. The viewer knows what no character knows.

Resolution options:
- **Never resolved** (most scripts) — the loop stays shut, which IS the payoff
- **Resolved by third party** — someone finds out accidentally (mother's slip, accidental phone notification)
- **Resolved by discovery** — friends discover and "things get weird"

**Device B: Mentor Aphorism — Planted at ~15%, Decoded at ~95%**
A mentor figure delivers ONE cryptic line early. That line becomes the video's structural key. It is explicitly decoded/revealed near the end.

Construction:
1. Mentor says the line in dialogue, casually, while doing something physical
2. Narrator says "You don't understand what he means yet. You will."
3. The line is NOT referenced again until 80%+ of runtime
4. At 88–95%, the narrator shows how the entire story IS the line

Verbatim examples:
| Planted | Decoded |
|---|---|
| "You won't feel rich doing this. That's how you know it's working." (3:29) | "He was right about the first part. He didn't mention the second half — that eventually you stop needing to feel it because the proof isn't a feeling anymore." (20:23) |
| "First, you stop the leak, then you dry the wall, then you paint." (early) | The entire level structure IS the three steps: Level 2 = stop the leak, Level 3 = dry the wall, Level 6+ = paint |

**Device C: The Foil's Deferred Bill**
Daniel's (or Christopher's) financial mistake NEVER receives commentary or judgement. It arrives as a single factual sentence, usually 5–10 minutes after the purchase was mentioned.

Verbatim:
- "The boat gets sold at a loss 6 weeks later for less than half of what he still owed on it."
- "He owes $29,000 on it. The best offer he's gotten is $24,500."
- "Four years later, he's still talking about the payment more than he talks about the truck."

**Device D: Drawdown Reframed as a Sale**
Every market crash beat uses the same mechanism: "While you're still contributing, a falling market isn't only a loss — it's a discount on everything you're about to buy."

Variant: "When a fund's price is down 25%, the same contribution buys a third more shares than it did in February."

**Device E: Loss vs. Price (Advanced — Used in Divorce/Rebuild Scripts)**
"The divorce took shares. Those shares were gone. The market didn't take a single share. One of those is a loss. The other is a price."

**Device F: The Cost Chapter**
At 78–88% of runtime, the story STOPS escalating and names what the discipline destroyed:
- Weddings missed
- Friendships that drifted
- "A language you used to be fluent in and have quietly forgotten"
- "The people who used to be exactly like you"
- The lakehouse photos with an empty chair

This is NOT a "but it was worth it" conclusion. The cost is REAL and NAMED. The viewer decides if it was worth it.

### 4.2 Re-hook Formula and Placement

**Type:** Always a negative-knowledge signpost. NEVER a question. NEVER a cliffhanger.

Four workhorse constructions:
1. "Here's what nobody tells you about [X]."
2. "That's the part nobody warns you about."
3. "Here's the strange part."
4. "That's the cost nobody puts in the wealth-building videos."

**Placement:**
- One in the hook
- Then one every 2.5–4 minutes
- Almost always at a level boundary or immediately after a balance figure
- **Density:** 6 per 21–23 minute script. Holds at 6–7 in 28–35 minute scripts — the interval stretches, the count does NOT increase

## SECTION 6 — PACING BLUEPRINT

### 6.1 Sentence Rhythm

The engine is **long sensory clause → short flat declarative.** Length contrast, not speed.

```
"You don't sell. You don't even seriously consider it."

"It's not life-changing. It's not supposed to be."

"It didn't feel like anything was happening, but something was."

"The account isn't down. The price is down."

"At $800, you used to panic. At $80,000, you just stand there."
```

### 6.2 Where It Breathes (Slow Down)

- **Immediately after every balance reveal** — a paragraph of nothing happening ("For the next eight months, nothing happens") + a physical-object beat
- **The cost chapter** — slows to one scene
- **The coda reprise** — deliberately slow, repeating exact details from the opening

### 6.3 Where It Sprints (Speed Up)

- **The raise-and-contribution sequence** — short consecutive sentences with numbers
- **The crash sequence** — numbers falling as standalone lines: "$176,000 / $159,000 / $131,880"
- **The final seven-figure montage** — rapid balance reveals across multiple years

### 6.4 Section Length Ratios

Per level chapter: **11–16% of runtime.**
Inside a chapter: roughly **60% scene, 20% mechanism, 20% montage/math.**

The mechanism paragraph appears in every script at the crossover, opening with:
- "Here's what compounding actually means."
- "Here's the mechanism underneath all of it."
- "That's the quiet shift nobody notices while it's happening."

## SECTION 7 — CHARACTER CONSTRUCTION

### 7.1 The Four Character Archetypes

| Archetype | Name (Reuse or Vary) | Function | Dialogue Quota |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Mentor** | Christopher, Daniel (mentor variant), Ashley (mentor variant), Walter, Denise | Delivers ONE aphorism early. Disappears. Returns at 88–95% for the decode. Retires before the viewer's crossover. | 1–2 lines total |
| **The Foil** | Daniel (primary, 14/14), Christopher (secondary foil in some scripts) | The spender. Makes sympathetic, understandable choices. Never stupid. Collapse stated in one factual line. | 1–2 lines total |
| **The Partner** | Jessica (primary), unnamed wife/husband | Discovers the number by accident. Asks the quiet question at 60%: "So, could you stop?" Opens her own Roth IRA. Never pushes. Never gives ultimatums. | 2–3 lines total |
| **The Witness** | Ashley (daughter), unnamed family, the boss, the plant | The person who misreads the viewer's life. Creates dramatic irony. "She looks at the truck and assumes." | 1–2 lines total |

### 7.2 Character Rules

1. **Daniel is ALWAYS the foil.** The name Daniel appears in 14/14 scripts. If you need a second foil, use Christopher
2. **The mentor is ALWAYS older** — 55+ years old, decades at the same job, quiet, observant
3. **The partner is NEVER antagonistic** — no arguments, no ultimatums, no "you need to spend more"
4. **The foil is NEVER mocked** — "He hugs you both at the end of the night and tells you it's the best backyard wedding he's been to. No exaggeration. He means it."
5. **Dialogue is aphoristic, never conversational** — Characters speak in proverbs, not sentences

### 7.3 Character Introduction Pattern

Every character is introduced with:
1. A physical description tied to the workplace
2. A tenure/history line ("61, 34 years at this company")
3. An observation about their personality in one clause ("The kind of quiet that makes people assume he isn't paying attention. He is.")
4. Their first and only meaningful line of dialogue

## SECTION 8 — CTA CONSTRUCTION

### 8.1 The Five-Move CTA Formula (14/14)

The CTA appears ONLY after the coda reprise. No mid-video ask. No mid-roll hard ask.

**Move 1: Conditional Story Callback**
```
"If any part of this looked familiar…"
"If this story stayed with you…"
"If this one hit home…"
```

**Move 2: Like + Reason**
```
"Like the video if it gave you something to think about today."
"Give it a like — it genuinely helps this channel keep making more of these."
```

**Move 3: Comment Question That Re-Opens the Video's Central Dilemma**
This is the craft move. It NEVER asks for generic engagement. It restates the video's own choice:
```
"Would you have bought the truck or kept the Civic?"
"If your money could cover your life tomorrow, would you still clock in or would you walk out the door?"
"If you had to rebuild from nothing, what would be your first step? Stop the leak, dry the wall, or something else entirely?"
"What's one thing you'd keep spending on, even if it pushed your freedom back a few years?"
"Is there something at work you'd say no to tomorrow if you knew you could afford the answer?"
```

**Move 4: Subscribe**
```
"Subscribe if you want more of these."
"Subscribe so you don't miss the next one."
```

**Move 5: Fixed Sign-Off**
```
"Thanks for spending this time with me. I'll see you in the next one."
```

Latest era addition: "I read those." (added to the comment question)

## SECTION 9 — THE RECURRING PHYSICAL OBJECT

### 9.1 Rules

1. The object appears in the FIRST 30 seconds (cold open)
2. It appears at LEAST 3 more times across the script
3. It appears in the CODA reprise (same object, same scene)
4. It is MUNDANE — never expensive, never impressive
5. It carries the THESIS — the object IS the viewer's value system made physical

### 9.2 Object Examples from the Corpus

| Object | Video | What It Represents |
|---|---|---|
| Scratched Tupperware container | "You Quietly Became a Millionaire" | 16 years of same lunch, same discipline, invisible wealth |
| Yellow legal pad | "You Lost Everything in the Divorce" | The column that starts with a minus sign and ends in six figures |
| Silver house key | "You Lost Everything in the Divorce" | What the viewer lost — tried in the lock at the end, doesn't turn |
| Thermos | "You Spent Less Than Everyone Else" | Frugality as identity, present from hospital cafeteria to the garden |
| Dented toolbox | Workplace scripts | The tools of the trade that outlast the career |
| Chipped mug | Various | Unchanged routine amid transformed finances |
| The fold of a pay stub | Various | The deduction nobody reads — the seed of everything |

### 9.3 How to Choose One

1. What does the viewer CARRY every day?
2. What would Daniel NEVER notice?
3. What could survive 16 years of the same routine?
4. What would Jessica see on the counter and not think twice about?

The object answers all four.

### 11.2 Latest Era (The "Then X" Construction)

```
POV: You [PAST TENSE SETUP] — Then [CONSEQUENCE]
```
Examples:
- "POV: You Became a Millionaire Overnight — Then Clocked In the Next Morning"
- "POV: Your Portfolio Started Paying More Than Your Job — Then Everything Changed"

### 11.3 Title Rules

1. Always starts with "POV:"
2. Always second person ("You")
3. Always past tense achievement or situation
4. Suffix creates curiosity gap (invisibility OR consequence)
5. Never mentions specific dollar amounts in the title
6. Never uses clickbait superlatives ("INSANE," "SHOCKING")

## SECTION 15 — SPEAKING PACE AND TTS NOTES

### 15.1 Narration Style

- **Pace:** Slow, deliberate, unhurried — approximately 150–155 WPM
- **Tone:** Calm, observational, never excited. Like a documentary narrator who is telling you something you already suspected
- **Emphasis:** Numbers get a beat of silence before and after. "The number was… one million forty-six thousand seven hundred dollars."
- **Dialogue:** Slightly different register — the mentor's lines are delivered as if overheard, not performed

### 15.2 TTS Preparation Rules

- All numbers written as spoken: "one million forty-six thousand seven hundred" not "$1,046,700"
- Paragraph breaks at every natural pause point
- No parenthetical asides (TTS flattens them)
- Dates expanded: "two thousand eight" not "2008"
- Abbreviations expanded: "four oh one k" not "401k"

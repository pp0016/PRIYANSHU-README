### 5.2 Visual Identity

**From the thumbnail forensics (8 laws extracted):**

1. **Recurring illustrated protagonist** — identical across all videos (brand asset, not decorative)
2. **Protagonist shows NO emotion** — neutral face. Supporting cast carries ALL emotion (smug, shocked, whispering, raging)
3. **Contrast rendered as OBJECTS, not words** — old car vs new car, tiny house vs mansion, packed lunch vs restaurant
4. **"POV" signal carried by composition** — camera angle implies first-person view; no "POV" text overlay needed
5. **Warm, semi-realistic, culturally American environments** — suburban homes, offices, parking lots, kitchen tables
6. **Numbers appear as diegetic UI only** — phone screens, receipts, bank statements. Never as headline text
7. **Numbers on diegetic surfaces only** — the one thumbnail with green `+780,000$` puts it inside the scene composition
8. **Supporting character casting is as important as the protagonist** — name their emotion when briefing thumbnails

**Stickman adaptation:** Your Nano Banana 2 stickman IS the recurring protagonist. Give it a consistent design (neutral white minimalist stickman with subtle charcoal outline) that becomes the channel's visual signature. The protagonist stays completely neutral; environment characters express intense emotion.

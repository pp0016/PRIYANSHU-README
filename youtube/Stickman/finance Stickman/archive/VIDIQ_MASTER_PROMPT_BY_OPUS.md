## THE PROMPT (Copy from here)

```text
I need you to do something specific and difficult. Read these instructions completely before you begin.

CONTEXT: I am building a faceless YouTube channel in the second-person POV wealth psychology niche (stealth wealth, quiet financial choices, life-path comparisons, system explainers). Target audience: Tier-1 English (US/UK/CA/AU), ages 18-35. Format: 2D illustrated stickman/POV narration, 12-20 minutes, natural human pacing with zero fluff.

WHAT I AM GIVING YOU: Four complete, unedited transcripts from real YouTube videos in this exact niche. Two are massive viral outliers from small channels. Two are underperformers/flops. Crucially, some of these come from the SAME channel or same niche position — meaning the voice, format, and audience are identical. The only variable is what the script did differently.

WHAT I AM NOT GIVING YOU: I am not giving you any pre-analysis, any theory about why the outliers worked, any hypothesis about what went wrong with the flops, any act structure, any percentage word budgets, any checklist, or any quality rubric. I am giving you nothing except raw transcripts and this task. Everything you produce must come from YOUR analysis of the actual text.

===================================================================
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT: WHAT YOU MUST NOT DO
===================================================================

- Do NOT produce generic YouTube advice ("make it engaging", "tell stories", "hook the viewer early"). If any sentence in your output could apply equally to a cooking channel, a gaming channel, and a finance channel, delete it.
- Do NOT invent theoretical act percentages (Act 1 = 18%, Act 2 = 23%, etc.). Real outlier scripts are topic-driven, not formula-locked. If the data shows a structural pattern, describe the PATTERN with evidence, not an artificial percentage table.
- Do NOT pre-judge the transcripts before analyzing them. I can tell when you're producing pre-cooked responses. Cite specific lines, specific phrases, specific moments from the actual transcripts to back every single claim.
- Do NOT produce a textbook. Produce a working document that a scriptwriter opens before every single writing session and uses as operational law.

===================================================================
YOUR TASK — SEVEN DELIVERABLES, IN THIS EXACT ORDER
===================================================================

You are not an assistant summarizing content. You are a veteran head of scriptwriting with decades of experience at a top-tier media company, and I just handed you four scripts from my team. Two crushed it. Two bombed. I need you to figure out exactly why, build the operating rules from what you find, and give me a system so tight that every future script passes through it before it ever reaches production.

Here is what I need, in this exact order:


### DELIVERABLE 1: THE OPENING 60-SECOND AUTOPSY (all 4 videos)

For each of the 4 transcripts, isolate the first ~200 words (approximately the first 60 seconds at conversational pace). Then answer:

- Quote the exact opening lines of each video verbatim.
- What emotional state does the viewer enter within 10 seconds? Name the specific emotion (not "curiosity" — that's too vague. Is it dread? Envy? Recognition? Voyeuristic tension? Self-identification?).
- Does the opening create a COGNITIVE CONTRACT with the viewer — a promise that if they stay, something specific will be resolved or revealed? What is that contract, stated in one sentence?
- For the flops: At what exact word or sentence does the opening lose the viewer? Quote it. What specifically goes wrong at that moment — does it shift from immersion to lecture? Does it break second person? Does it introduce abstraction before stakes?

Do not generalize. Quote the text.


### DELIVERABLE 2: FORENSIC FULL-SCRIPT CONTRAST

Now analyze the complete transcripts, not just the openings. Compare the outliers against the flops across these dimensions. For each dimension, cite at least one specific quote from an outlier AND one from a flop:

**A. Perspective and immersion mechanics:**
- How do the outliers maintain second-person ("you") immersion without it becoming repetitive or gimmicky? At what points do they shift perspective, and why does it work?
- Where do the flops break immersion? Quote the exact moment each flop stops being about the viewer and starts being about the creator or an abstract concept.

**B. How financial information is delivered:**
- The outliers contain real financial numbers ($18/hour, $52,000, $200/month Roth IRA, 8% returns). How do they embed this data without ever sounding like a textbook or lecture?
- Quote the specific structural technique the outliers use to wrap numbers inside narrative (e.g., do they attach a number to a physical sensation? A social consequence? A time-stamped life moment?).
- Quote where the flops deliver information in a way that creates drag or sounds didactic.

**C. Tension and stakes architecture:**
- Map the tension curve of each outlier: where does tension rise, where does it plateau, where does it get refreshed? What mechanism refreshes it?
- What do the outliers do to make the viewer feel that something is AT STAKE for THEM personally — not just intellectually interesting?
- What is completely absent from the flops in terms of stakes?

**D. Transition mechanics:**
- Quote 3 transitions from the outliers. How does each section hand off to the next without announced structure ("next", "point two", "let's move on")?
- Quote transitions from the flops. What goes wrong?

**E. Ending and payoff:**
- How do the outliers end? Do they resolve the opening tension? Do they hand the viewer off to a new question?
- How do the flops end? What is missing?


### DELIVERABLE 3: NON-SCRIPT ROOT CAUSE DIAGNOSIS

This is critical. For EACH of the two flop videos, answer this independently:

Was the script itself the primary reason this video underperformed? Or was it a potentially viable script that was killed by something outside the script?

Consider and diagnose each of these factors separately:
- **Title-to-content contract:** Does the title create an expectation that the script fails to fulfill? Is there a broken cognitive contract between what the viewer clicked on and what they got?
- **Premise strength:** Is the core concept too narrow, too niche, too personal, or too abstract to attract a broad audience in the first place?
- **Target audience mismatch:** Is this script talking to the wrong viewer? (e.g., already-converted minimalists vs. curious outsiders)
- **Cultural resonance and shareability:** Does this concept trigger the "I need to send this to someone" instinct, or is it purely self-referential?
- **Pacing and energy:** Even if the ideas are good, does the delivery create fatigue through monotonous rhythm, lack of variation, or excessive length on single points?

State your verdict clearly for each flop: "The script was the problem" OR "The script was viable but [X] killed it" OR "Both the script and [X] failed simultaneously."


### DELIVERABLE 4: THE MASTER SCRIPTWRITER SOP

Based on ONLY what you found in Deliverables 1-3 — not from any external framework or textbook — write a complete Scriptwriter Standard Operating Procedure for this channel.

Write it as if you are the head of a scriptwriting department handing this document to a new hire. It should be specific enough that someone who has never seen our channel can follow it and produce a script that sounds like it belongs.

The SOP must cover, at minimum:
- How to choose a topic that has viral structural potential (based on what the outliers have in common)
- How to open a script (with specific do/don't rules derived from what you found)
- How to deliver financial information without creating lecture drag
- How to maintain immersion and perspective discipline
- How to create and refresh tension across a 12-20 minute runtime
- How to transition between sections without announced structure
- How to end a script so the viewer feels both resolved and hungry for more
- What specific patterns, phrases, structures, and approaches are PERMANENTLY BANNED based on what killed the flops

Do not produce platitudes. Every rule must be traceable to a specific finding from the transcript analysis.


### DELIVERABLE 5: THE UNIVERSAL VIRAL SCRIPT FORMAT

Extract a repeatable narrative structure — not a rigid percentage table, but a flexible beat sequence — that the outlier scripts share. Describe each beat by:
- Its FUNCTION (what it does for the viewer psychologically)
- Its approximate POSITION in the script (early, mid, late — not a percentage)
- A QUOTED EXAMPLE from one of the outlier transcripts showing what it looks like in practice
- A COUNTER-EXAMPLE from a flop showing what happens when this beat is missing or broken

This format should be something I can print on one page and pin above my desk.


### DELIVERABLE 6: THE QUALITY GATE — PASS/FAIL SCORING RUBRIC

Create an objective, no-mercy scoring system to evaluate any future script BEFORE it goes to voiceover production. The gate must have two components:

**Component A: Must-Pass Criteria (all required)**
List the specific markers that MUST be present for a script to proceed. Each criterion must be stated as a binary yes/no question that a reviewer can answer by reading the script. Derive these directly from what the outliers consistently do.

**Component B: Instant Disqualification Triggers**
List the specific fatal errors that AUTOMATICALLY REJECT a script, no matter how good the rest of it is. Derive these directly from the specific mistakes found in the flops. State each trigger as: "If the script [does X], it fails. Evidence: [quote from flop where this killed the video]."


### DELIVERABLE 7: TITLE-TO-SCRIPT ALIGNMENT TEST

For each of the 4 videos, score the alignment between the title's promise and the script's delivery on a 1-10 scale:
- What did the title promise the viewer would experience?
- Did the script deliver on that promise within the first 60 seconds?
- Did the script fully resolve the title's implied question by the end?
- Where specifically did any misalignment occur?

Then provide 5 operational rules for how to write titles for future scripts in this niche, based on what the high-performing title/script pairings did right.


===================================================================
MANDATORY OUTPUT FORMAT
===================================================================

Compile and output the entire dossier strictly as a complete, fully structured, publishable document formatted with clean Markdown headers (#, ##, ###), bold lead-ins for every paragraph, quoted transcript excerpts in blockquotes, and full data tables where comparisons are made. Every claim must cite a specific transcript quote. No section may contain fewer than 3 paragraphs. The document must be ready for immediate export and permanent use as a channel operating standard — not a chat response, not a summary, not a conversation. A document.
```

```text
Based strictly on the emotional core, central conflict, and psychological payoff of this script:

   - BROKEN SYSTEM (4 titles): "The [hidden mechanism] keeping most people [stuck state]"
   - UNSPOKEN MISTAKE (3 titles): "The $[amount] mistake nobody talks about"

   For each: predicted vidIQ score, why it creates curiosity without cheap clickbait, and which specific script moment it hooks into.

2. Write a 2-paragraph video description in second-person POV. Naturally weave in high-volume search keywords without stuffing. Include 3 timestamp chapter labels keyed to the script's emotional turning points.

3. Provide 15 YouTube tags (mix of broad niche + specific long-tail) and 3 primary keyword clusters.
```

```text
You are a ruthless retention auditor. I am about to send this script to voiceover production. Before I spend resources, tell me if it will work.

TITLE: [INSERT SELECTED TITLE]
QUALITY GATE: [PASTE THE QUALITY GATE FROM THE MASTER PROMPT OUTPUT — Deliverable 6]

SCRIPT:
[PASTE DRAFT SCRIPT]

Answer with zero sugarcoating:

1. VIRALITY SCORE (0-100): Score this script's breakout potential purely on narrative mechanics, emotional resonance, and retention architecture. Can this script break out on a channel under 10K subscribers? Why or why not?

2. DRAG CHECK: Quote every specific line, paragraph, or transition that creates viewer fatigue, sounds like generic advice, breaks second-person immersion, or lectures. For each, write the replacement line.

3. TITLE-SCRIPT CONTRACT: Does the first 60 seconds honor the emotional promise of the title? Does the viewer get hooked on the central question before any dry data appears? If not, where does the contract break?

4. QUALITY GATE PASS/FAIL: Run the script against every criterion in the Quality Gate. List each criterion and mark PASS or FAIL. If any Instant Disqualification Trigger fires, state which one and quote the offending line.

5. VERDICT:
   [PASS FOR PRODUCTION] — Ready for voiceover.
   OR
   [REVISE BEFORE PRODUCTION] — List the exact edits needed to pass. No more than 5. Be specific enough that I can make the fix in under 10 minutes.
```

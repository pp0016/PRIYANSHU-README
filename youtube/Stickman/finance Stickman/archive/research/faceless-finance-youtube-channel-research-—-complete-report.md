## 0. How to read this report — evidence standards

Before any numbers, here is what every claim in this report rests on, because the confidence is not uniform.

| Evidence tier | What it means | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **Verified this session** | Pulled directly from a tool result during this research, and the figure reconcilable | Channel metrics, per-video view counts, publish dates, durations |
| **Visually verified** | Confirmed by watching the video (first 30 min max for long videos) | The Visual Format column |
| **Calculated** | Derived by arithmetic from verified inputs, and labelled as such | Average video length, lifetime avg views/video |
| **Not re-verified** | Reported earlier from a source that this session's data could not reconcile | Called out explicitly in the Corrections Log |
| **Hypothesis** | A plausible reading of the data, not measured | All "why" explanations |

Two measurement cautions that apply throughout:

- **View counts are cumulative lifetime totals**, not views gained in any window. A video published 12 days ago and a video published 12 months ago are not comparable on views alone.
- **"Average views per video" is a lifetime average** (total channel views ÷ total video count). It is not current performance, and a channel that peaked years ago will carry an inflated average. This distinction turns out to matter enormously — see Section 4.

## 5. Revised answer — Question 1: What do the 200K+ channels do differently from the 50K–100K ones?

Five differences show up in the verified data. They are ranked by how strong the evidence is.

### 5.1 Topic scope: they cover things that happen TO money, not things you do WITH money

This is the strongest and most consistent difference.

**200K+ framing (verified 1M+ performers):**
- "Australia May Have Just Solved The Housing Crisis" — Economics Explained, 1,542,036
- "Why Are We Doing This To Canada?" — How Money Works, 1,530,042
- "The New American Housing Crisis Has Landed" — Casual Finance, 2,365,068
- "Why Only Three Countries Bother Building Ships Anymore" — Micro, 3,205,024
- "How A Single Costco Changes Its Local Economy" — Micro, 3,067,781
- "How Did America Pay Off The National Debt Last Time?" — How Business Worked, 1,143,093

**50K–100K framing (recent performers):**
- "Why The Wealthy NEVER Use Joint Bank Accounts" — Bob Invests, 8,961
- "Dividends vs Growth: The 25-Year Experiment" — Bob Invests, 7,153
- "10 Boring Habits That Build More Wealth Than Any Side Hustle" — Bob Invests, 6,032
- "Why the Wealthy Never Say 'I'm Sorry'" — The Luxury Lane, 5,735
- "Why Rich People Never Show Off (10 Old Money Habits)" — The Luxury Lane, 4,758

The 200K+ group frames episodes around a country, institution, company or live event where the stakes belong to everyone. The 50K–100K group in this band sits mostly in the personal-habits lane, which is both far more crowded and structurally capped — a video about a habit competes with every other habit video, while a video about Australia's housing policy competes with almost nothing.

**The important nuance:** this is not a subscriber-size effect, it's a *framing* effect, and the small channels that adopt the big framing win. Schmancy's 201,022-view "11 MANHATTAN BUILDINGS You'll Never Get Into" and Stealth Wealth's 180,231-view "12 Things You Only Notice in Upper Class Homes" are both *observation* frames — specific, named, inaccessible subjects — not advice frames. Both broke out well above their channel's norm.

### 5.2 A named, repeatable franchise

The 200K+ group runs franchises. This is verifiable from titles alone:

| Franchise | Channel | Verified entries |
|---|---|---|
| "___ Explained with Bananas" | Primate Economics | 18+ entries; Tax 1,605,463; Tariffs 1.5M; Credit Cards 1,214,551; Bubbles 1,128,764; Great Depression 917,242; Crypto 853,782 |

| "___ in 12 Minutes" | Casual Economics | Great Depression 3,119,590; 2008 Crisis 408,438; Japan's Lost Decade 205,000 |

The 50K–100K channels largely do not. Bob Invests, The Luxury Lane and Stealth Wealth publish one-off listicles with rotating numbers ("10 Things…", "12 Things…", "15 Things…"), which is a *format* but not a *franchise* — there's no shared promise carrying a viewer from one video to the next.

### 5.3 Cadence, not volume

Verified publish dates:
- Economics Explained: roughly weekly, some 4-day gaps
- How Money Works: weekly, no gaps in the sampled window
- Casual Finance: multiple longform per week
- Epic Economist: near-daily, sometimes several per day
- Practical Wisdom: near-daily
- Micro: roughly monthly

**Volume alone does not work.** Epic Economist publishes near-daily across 880 videos and its recent ceiling is 69K. Practical Wisdom publishes near-daily across 1,575 videos and its recent ceiling is 22.6K. Stealth Wealth has 637 videos and an 8,497 average. Volume without a differentiated, franchise-shaped promise just distributes the same ceiling across more uploads. Meanwhile Primate Economics has **29 videos** and a 1,107,627 average.

### 5.5 A human editorial voice as a character

Every 200K+ channel in this set runs a consistent, authored voiceover with a recognisable personality — Economics Explained's measured lecturer, How Money Works' exasperated "WTF" narrator, Primate's deadpan description of finance through bananas, How Business Worked's sarcastic caps-lock framing. I cannot measure *why* any of it works, and topic choice and viewing demand are not the same thing. The causal stories above are hypotheses consistent with the data, not findings the data proves.

## 8. Methodology — how every figure in this report was produced

**Channel discovery.** Seven parallel channel-index searches using your exact queries ("finance explained", "how money works", "economic collapse", "wealth psychology", "dark finance", "money stories animated", "financial scandals explained"), each filtered to faceless channels with a 50,000+ subscriber floor. Follow-up searches on related terms surfaced the six additional channels in Section 7.

**Channel metrics.** Subscriber counts, lifetime views, video counts and publish dates were pulled per channel from live channel data. Subscriber figures from the channel index are periodically rebuilt rather than live, which is why a few counts differ by under 1% from the index (Section 1.4).

**Per-video data.** View counts, likes, comments, engagement rates, durations and publish dates came from two sources: a per-channel top-video pull and a best-of-window pull. An important limit: **the per-channel top-video pull returns a recent window, not lifetime top videos.** That is why Economics Explained's top-video list does not include a video from October 2025. Where a lifetime best-of set was available (Economics Explained, Primate Economics) it is labelled as such. Where it was not, the "top video" in Section 3 should be read as "top video in the fetched window."

**Visual verification.** The Visual Format column was confirmed by watching actual footage — for long videos, the opening 30 minutes at most, with the analysis result reporting its own coverage. This is a sample of one video per channel, not a catalogue-wide review. Where a visual check produced no usable result, the table says so rather than guessing. All sixteen channels in this report now have verified visual formats except the six newly-added channels in Section 7, whose inclusion rests on the channel index's faceless flag and their own descriptions.

**Calculated values.** "Average video length" and "avg views/video" are arithmetic means over the sampled videos and are marked as calculated everywhere they appear. They are not tool-reported metrics.

**Outlier search.** Separate outlier searches on "economic collapse", "financial scandal fraud", "housing crisis", "personal finance wealth mindset" and "money explainer animated" returned per-video view counts, breakout scores (a multiple of the channel's average views — a ratio, not a rate or a forecast), views per hour and channel size, with publish ages in days.

**Keyword demand.** One country-level keyword pull for the US around "economic collapse" returned in-country search volumes: finance (509,768), personal finance (192,285), stock market (68,490), economy (68,203), economics (55,661), federal reserve (43,444). Useful context: the highest-volume *specific* finance terms are broad category words, not collapse terms — which is consistent with the pattern that specific country and institution stories outperform generic collapse framing.

**What this research cannot tell you.** It cannot tell you click-through rate or impressions (not available through these tools), retention curves, or revenue. It cannot prove causation for any pattern here. It cannot tell you a topic will perform for *your* channel — only how it has performed for these channels.

## 9. What to do with this

If you are building or reshaping a faceless finance channel, the data points at five things in priority order:

1. **Pick the country/institution case-study format.** It is the only format in this dataset with 1M+ verified hits across three or more channels. The frame is: a specific country or company, a live stakes-driven question, a concrete answer.
2. **Build a named franchise, not a series of one-offs.** "___ Explained with Bananas" produced 18+ entries and multiple 1M+ videos from 29 total uploads. "___ in 12 Minutes" produced a 3.1M video from an 11-video channel.
3. **Judge channels by per-video views in the last 90 days, not subscribers.** Casual Economics (92,600 subs) outperforms Practical Wisdom (1,040,000 subs) by an order of magnitude per video.
4. **Do not confuse volume with progress.** Near-daily publishing across 1,575 videos produced a recent ceiling of 22.6K views. Twenty-nine videos produced a 1.1M average.
5. **Do not overwrite runtime.** Both the best and worst performers run long. It is not the lever.

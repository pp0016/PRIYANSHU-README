Once you have the Master Quality Gate from the prompt above, you only need these two prompts for daily production:

### Daily Prompt A: Script-First Title + SEO (10 credits, per video)

```text
Here is my finished script for a faceless POV wealth psychology video (second-person, stickman-illustrated, 12-20 min, Tier-1 English audience 18-35):

[PASTE FINISHED SCRIPT]

Based strictly on the emotional core, central conflict, and psychological payoff of this script:

1. Generate 10 title candidates across these 3 categories:
   - STEALTH PARADOX (3 titles): "POV: You quietly [action] while everyone [contrast]"
   - BROKEN SYSTEM (4 titles): "The [hidden mechanism] keeping most people [stuck state]"
   - UNSPOKEN MISTAKE (3 titles): "The $[amount] mistake nobody talks about"

   For each: predicted vidIQ score, why it creates curiosity without cheap clickbait, and which specific script moment it hooks into.
   Mark your #1 recommendation.

2. Write a 2-paragraph video description in second-person POV. Naturally weave in high-volume search keywords without stuffing. Include 3 timestamp chapter labels keyed to the script's emotional turning points.

3. Provide 15 YouTube tags (mix of broad niche + specific long-tail) and 3 primary keyword clusters.
```

### Daily Prompt B: Pre-Flight Script Audit (10 credits, per video)

```text
You are a ruthless retention auditor. I am about to send this script to voiceover production. Before I spend resources, tell me if it will work.

TITLE: [INSERT SELECTED TITLE]
QUALITY GATE: [PASTE THE QUALITY GATE FROM THE MASTER PROMPT OUTPUT — Deliverable 6]

SCRIPT:
[PASTE DRAFT SCRIPT]

Answer with zero sugarcoating:

1. VIRALITY SCORE (0-100): Score this script's breakout potential purely on narrative mechanics, emotional resonance, and retention architecture. Can this script break out on a channel under 10K subscribers? Why or why not?

2. DRAG CHECK: Quote every specific line, paragraph, or transition that creates viewer fatigue, sounds like generic advice, breaks second-person immersion, or lectures. For each, write the replacement line.

3. TITLE-SCRIPT CONTRACT: Does the first 60 seconds honor the emotional promise of the title? Does the viewer get hooked on the central question before any dry data appears? If not, where does the contract break?

4. QUALITY GATE PASS/FAIL: Run the script against every criterion in the Quality Gate. List each criterion and mark PASS or FAIL. If any Instant Disqualification Trigger fires, state which one and quote the offending line.

5. VERDICT:
   [PASS FOR PRODUCTION] — Ready for voiceover.
   OR
   [REVISE BEFORE PRODUCTION] — List the exact edits needed to pass. No more than 5. Be specific enough that I can make the fix in under 10 minutes.
```

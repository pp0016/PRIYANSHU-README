## Primary Sources

Academic, legal, and historical sources for fact-checking the script. NOT YouTube scripts.

| Source | Type | What It Covers | Why It Matters |
|--------|------|---------------|---------------|
| ICRC — IHL Treaty Database (ihl-databases.icrc.org) | Legal database | Full text of every IHL treaty, protocol, and convention | The actual legal language. Cross-check any "banned by X treaty" claim here. |
| St. Petersburg Declaration (1868) | Treaty text | Ban on explosive projectiles under 400g | First formal international treaty to ban a specific weapon. The founding document. |
| Hague Declarations (1899) | Treaty text | Expanding bullets, asphyxiating gases, aerial bombardment from balloons | Three separate declarations — most videos conflate them into one. |
| Hague Regulations (1907) | Treaty text | Codified laws of war including poison/poisoned weapons | Broadened 1899 scope. Article 23 is the key article. |
| Geneva Protocol (1925) | Treaty text | Chemical and biological warfare ban | Post-WWI response. Only banned USE, not production or stockpiling. |
| Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (1980) | Treaty + 5 Protocols | Non-detectable fragments (I), mines/booby-traps (II), incendiary weapons (III), blinding lasers (IV), explosive remnants (V) | The "umbrella" convention most videos ignore. Each protocol is a separate story. |
| Ottawa Treaty (1997) | Treaty text | Anti-personnel landmine ban | US, Russia, China NOT signatories — the biggest paradox in modern arms control. |
| Chemical Weapons Convention (1993) | Treaty text | Comprehensive chemical weapons ban + riot control agent exemption | The tear gas paradox lives here: Article II(9)(d). |
| Convention on Cluster Munitions (2008) | Treaty text | Cluster bomb ban | Same non-signatories problem as Ottawa. |
| UN Office for Disarmament Affairs (unoda.org) | Government source | Current treaty status, signatories, ratification data | For the "who signed and who didn't" comparisons. |
| SIPRI (sipri.org) | Research institute | Arms trade data, military expenditure, weapons development | For any "how much did this cost" claims. |
| Laws of Manu / Manusmriti (Ch. 7, v. 90) | Ancient text | Hindu code of *Dharmayuddha* — banned barbed, poison, fire arrows ~1500 BCE | Predates European arms control by 3,000+ years. Powerful "this isn't new" angle. |
| Second Lateran Council (1139) | Church decree | Crossbow ban (Canon 29) | The class warfare angle: peasant vs. knight economics. |
| Strasbourg Agreement (1675) | Treaty text | Poison-coated musket ball ban (France + Holy Roman Empire) | First chemical arms control treaty — 250 years before Geneva. Almost nobody covers this. |
| Outer Space Treaty (1967) | Treaty text | Bans nuclear/biological/chemical weapons in orbit — NOT conventional weapons | The "Rods from God" loophole: kinetic bombardment is technically legal. |

## Weapon Longlist (All Candidates)

Every weapon banned, restricted, or debated under international law or custom.

### Ancient (pre-1500)

| Weapon | What It Does | Banned/Restricted By | Surprising Fact |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Poison arrows** | Coat arrowheads with plant/animal toxins | Laws of Manu (~1500 BCE), Greek/Roman custom, Hague Regs Art. 23(a) | India had formalized weapon ethics 3,000 years before Europe. The Manusmriti also banned attacking sleeping soldiers and poisoning water wells. |
| **Barbed arrows** | Designed to lodge in flesh, causing more damage on removal | Laws of Manu (~1500 BCE) | The ban wasn't mercy — it was about honor. *Dharmayuddha* (righteous war) demanded warriors face each other as equals. |
| **Fire arrows** | Incendiary arrows designed to burn structures/formations | Laws of Manu (~1500 BCE), various medieval codes | Fire was considered a weapon of siege, not honorable combat. |
| **Crossbow** | Mechanical bow firing bolts that penetrate plate armor | Second Lateran Council Canon 29 (1139) | Banned against Christians, LEGAL against Muslims and "heretics." Real reason: a peasant with 2 hours of training could kill a knight who cost a lord a fortune to equip. The ban was economic, not moral. |
| **Greek fire** | Incendiary liquid that burns on water, projected from siphons | Church attempted ban (various medieval edicts) | The formula was a Byzantine state secret so closely guarded it was LOST. Modern chemists believe it was petroleum + resin + sulfur — essentially medieval napalm. |

### Early Modern (1500-1800)

| Weapon | What It Does | Banned/Restricted By | Surprising Fact |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Poisoned musket balls** | Musket balls coated in sulfur, arsenic, or other toxins | Strasbourg Agreement (1675) — France + Holy Roman Empire | First chemical arms control treaty in modern history. Predates the Geneva Convention by 250 years. Almost no competitor video covers this. |
| **Chain shot / bar shot** | Two cannonballs connected by chain, spins through rigging/personnel | Informal "gentlemen's agreements" — debated but never formally banned | Designed to shred ship rigging and dismember sailors on deck. The spinning action was psychologically devastating. |

### Industrial / WWI (1800-1918)

| Weapon | What It Does | Banned/Restricted By | Surprising Fact |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Explosive bullets (under 400g)** | Small-caliber bullets with explosive charges | St. Petersburg Declaration (1868) | This is the founding treaty of modern arms control. 17 nations agreed that the *only* legitimate object of war is to weaken *military forces* — not to maximize individual suffering. |
| **Expanding ("dum-dum") bullets** | Soft-nose/hollow-point bullets that mushroom on impact, creating massive exit wounds | Hague Declaration III (1899) | Named after the Dum Dum Arsenal near Kolkata, India, where British forces manufactured them. Banned in war but LEGAL for every police department on Earth. |
| **Poison / poisoned weapons** | Any weapon designed to deliver toxins | Hague Regulations Art. 23(a) (1907), codifying ancient customs | The modern codification of a rule older than written law — poisoning wells and weapons was taboo in virtually every ancient culture. |
| **Asphyxiating gases** | Projectiles designed to spread poisonous/asphyxiating gas | Hague Declaration II (1899) | Germany argued their chlorine gas was released from *canisters*, not *projectiles*, so it didn't violate the Declaration. Technically, they were right. |
| **Trench shotgun (Winchester Model 1897)** | Pump-action shotgun with slam-fire capability — hold trigger, pump to fire | Germany formally protested (1918), never actually banned | Germany threatened to EXECUTE captured American soldiers carrying shotguns — while simultaneously deploying mustard gas and flamethrowers. US Secretary of State Lansing told them to pound sand. The shotgun remains legal to this day. |
| **Chemical weapons (mustard gas, chlorine, phosgene)** | Blistering agents, choking agents, nerve agents | Geneva Protocol (1925) | The Geneva Protocol only banned USE — nations could still produce, stockpile, and develop new chemical weapons. It took until the CWC (1993) to ban production. |

### WWII Era (1918-1945)

| Weapon | What It Does | Banned/Restricted By | Surprising Fact |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Biological weapons** | Weaponized pathogens (anthrax, plague, smallpox) | Geneva Protocol (1925), Biological Weapons Convention (1972) | Japan's Unit 731 conducted live human experiments with bioweapons during WWII. The US gave the researchers IMMUNITY in exchange for their data. None were prosecuted. |
| **Bat bomb (Project X-Ray)** | Canister of 1,000+ hibernating bats, each carrying a timed napalm incendiary device | Never deployed; cancelled 1944 | In a test at Carlsbad Army Airfield, bats escaped and burned down the base's own control tower. The project was cancelled in favor of the Manhattan Project — not because it didn't work, but because the atomic bomb was faster. |

### Modern / Cold War (1945-2000)

| Weapon | What It Does | Banned/Restricted By | Surprising Fact |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Non-detectable fragments** | Weapons that injure via glass, plastic, or other X-ray invisible shrapnel | CCW Protocol I (1980) | Designed to make wounds impossible to treat. Surgeons can't find what they can't see. This is the only weapons ban based entirely on medical impossibility. |
| **Booby-traps disguised as civilian objects** | Explosive devices concealed in toys, food, medical supplies, etc. | CCW Protocol II (1980, amended 1996) | The Soviet "butterfly mine" (PFM-1) dropped in millions over Afghanistan resembled a green plastic toy. Children picked them up. |
| **Incendiary weapons (air-delivered near civilians)** | Weapons designed to set fires/cause burns (napalm, thermite, WP) | CCW Protocol III (1980) | White phosphorus is technically classified as a "smoke-screening agent," NOT an incendiary weapon — so it's LEGAL despite burning at 1,500°F and causing bone-deep chemical burns. The loophole is in the classification, not the physics. |
| **Blinding laser weapons** | Lasers designed to permanently blind the naked eye | CCW Protocol IV (1995) | The ONLY weapon in history banned BEFORE it was ever used in combat. Blinding as "collateral" from targeting lasers? Perfectly legal. |
| **Anti-personnel landmines** | Victim-activated explosive devices | Ottawa Treaty (1997) | Over 60 million mines still buried globally. The US, Russia, China, India, Pakistan — the world's largest militaries — have NOT signed the ban. |
| **Tear gas (riot control agents)** | CS gas, pepper spray — cause temporary blindness, breathing difficulty, pain | CWC (1993) — banned in WAR, legal for POLICE | You cannot spray tear gas at enemy soldiers under international law. You CAN spray it at your own citizens. The exemption is Article II(9)(d) of the CWC. |

### Contemporary (2000-Present)

| Weapon | What It Does | Banned/Restricted By | Surprising Fact |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Cluster munitions** | Air-dropped bombs that scatter hundreds of smaller "bomblets" across a wide area | Convention on Cluster Munitions (2008) | Up to 40% of bomblets fail to detonate on impact, becoming de facto landmines. The US, Russia, China refuse to sign. |

### Currently Debated (Not Yet Banned)

| Weapon | What It Does | Current Status | Surprising Fact |
|--------|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Lethal Autonomous Weapons (LAWS) / "killer robots"** | AI-powered systems that select and engage targets without human intervention | UN GGE discussions ongoing. 156 nations voted for a binding treaty in Nov 2025. US and Russia blocking. | The central legal problem: if an AI commits a war crime, who goes to prison? The programmer? The commander? The manufacturer? Nobody? This is the "accountability gap." |
| **"Rods from God" (kinetic bombardment)** | Tungsten rods dropped from orbit, hitting at Mach 10+ with the force of a tactical nuke — no explosive warhead | Outer Space Treaty (1967) bans nukes in orbit but NOT conventional weapons. Technically legal. | A 6m tungsten rod hitting at orbital velocity delivers the energy of a small nuclear weapon with zero radiation and zero warning. The Outer Space Treaty has a loophole you could fly a tungsten rod through. |
| **Thermobaric weapons** | Fuel-air explosives that create a pressure wave followed by a vacuum — ruptures lungs, collapses buildings | No specific treaty ban. Regulated only by general IHL principles. | The weapon creates a pressure wave that kills everyone in an enclosed space by rupturing their internal organs, then creates a vacuum that collapses the structure. Used widely, never specifically banned. |
| **Cyber weapons** | Malware, EMP devices, infrastructure attacks targeting power grids, water, hospitals | No binding treaty. Tallinn Manual (2013/2017) provides non-binding guidance. | There is no "Geneva Convention for cyber warfare." A nation could theoretically shut down another nation's hospital grid and argue it violated no treaty. |

## Overused Facts to Skip or Twist

These appear in nearly every banned weapons video. If you spend more than 15 seconds on any of these, viewers will click off.

1. **"Dum-dum bullets expand on impact" + Hague 1899 explanation.** Every. Single. Video. → **TWIST:** Don't explain what they do (everyone knows). Instead, lead with the paradox: "Your local police carry ammunition that's illegal in war." Make it about the legal contradiction, not the ballistics.

2. **"The Pope banned crossbows in 1139."** Simple History has 4M views on this alone. → **TWIST:** 15 seconds max. Focus on the CLASS WARFARE angle (peasant vs. knight economics), not the papal decree. The decree is the surface fact. The economics are the insight.

3. **"Mustard gas was horrible in WWI."** Textbook content. → **TWIST:** Speed through gas in under 60 seconds. Use it as a BRIDGE: "The horror of WWI gas led to the 1925 Geneva Protocol — but that Protocol only banned USE, not production. Nations kept making gas for another 68 years."

4. **"Blinding lasers were banned before they were used."** Interesting fact but overexposed. → **TWIST:** Pair with the "incidental blindness" loophole. The ban has an asterisk: if the blindness is a side effect of targeting equipment, it's legal. The ban is narrower than it sounds.

5. **"Landmines kill civilians decades after wars end" + Princess Diana advocacy.** → **TWIST:** Skip Princess Diana entirely. Instead: show the map of 60M still-buried mines, then the list of non-signatories. The twist is that the countries with the most landmines didn't sign the ban.

6. **"White phosphorus is technically legal because it's classified as a smoke screen."** Increasingly common in newer videos. → **TWIST:** If this is becoming overused, speed through it. Focus on the MECHANISM of the loophole (classification language in CCW Protocol III), not just the "it burns" shock value.

## Fresh Angles (Our Differentiators)

These are the stories, paradoxes, and perspectives that NO competitor video covers. These are what make the video feel human-made.

### 1. The Timeline Inversion — "Humans Agreed on Weapon Ethics 3,000 Years Before Geneva"
**Angle:** Open the video's intellectual frame with the Laws of Manu (~1500 BCE). India had codified bans on barbed arrows, poison arrows, fire arrows, and attacking sleeping soldiers millennia before the Hague Convention. The Strasbourg Agreement (1675) was the first *European* chemical arms treaty — but not the first *human* one. **Why it works:** Reframes "banned weapons" from a European legal history story to a HUMAN NATURE story. Every competitor starts with the Hague (1899). You start with ancient India.

### 2. The "Loophole Museum" — How Every Ban Has a Backdoor
**Angle:** Instead of just listing bans, show how each one is undermined by its own legal language:
- Tear gas: banned in war, legal on citizens (CWC Article II(9)(d))
- White phosphorus: burns at 1,500°F but classified as "smoke"
- Expanding bullets: war crime on a battlefield, standard issue for police
- Blinding lasers: banned as primary function, legal as "incidental" effect
- Nuclear weapons: legal in war. Expanding bullets: illegal.
**Why it works:** This turns the video from a list into an ARGUMENT. "The rules of war aren't about mercy — they're about what powerful countries are willing to give up."

### 3. The "Weapons That WEREN'T Banned" Counter-List
**Angle:** Every competitor video covers what's banned. NOBODY covers what ISN'T:
- Flamethrowers: restricted but legal. Burns people alive — somehow not banned.
- Thermobaric weapons: rupture lungs and collapse buildings — no specific treaty.
- Nuclear weapons: can vaporize a city — technically legal under most frameworks.
- Then pivot: "Expanding bullets are a war crime. Nuclear weapons are legal. How?"
**Why it works:** The contrast between banned and not-banned is MORE disturbing than the banned weapons themselves. This is the paradox hook from the phase1 research, deployed as a recurring thread.

### 4. The Non-Western Origins Story
**Angle:** Every competitor frames weapons law as a European invention (Hague → Geneva → CCW). But:
- Laws of Manu (~1500 BCE): India
- *Dharmayuddha* (righteous war): prohibited attacking at night, poisoning water, using weapons against non-combatants
- Islamic law of armed conflict: protection of non-combatants, prohibition of mutilation, rules on treatment of prisoners
**Why it works:** Breaks the "history = Europe" bias that plagues every competitor. Also taps into a massive Indian audience (Hindi comments, high engagement).

### 5. The "Enforcement Is a Myth" Thread
**Angle:** Don't end on a hopeful note. End on an honest one: most bans are unenforceable. The nations with the most banned weapons refuse to sign the bans. The ICJ has no enforcement mechanism. The UN GGE works on consensus (one country can veto). **Pair this with the autonomous weapons closer:** "And now we're debating whether to ban robot soldiers — using the same system that can't stop countries from burying landmines."
**Why it works:** This is the "cynical but true" angle that makes the video feel written by a human who actually thought about this, not an AI that listed treaties.

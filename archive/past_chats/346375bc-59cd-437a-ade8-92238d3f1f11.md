### ANSWER
Applying the 80/20 rule to English grammar, these six errors account for most professional writing mistakes that bypass native speaker intuition. 

1. **Object Hypercorrection ("I" vs. "Me")**
   - **Error:** "The client sent the feedback to John and I."
   - **Correct:** "...to John and me."
   - **Test:** Remove the other person from the sentence. You would not say "The client sent the feedback to I."

2. **The "Who" vs. "Whom" Split**
   - **Error:** "Whom is managing this?" (Incorrect subject) or "Who should I give this to?" (Informal object).
   - **Correct:** "Who is managing this?" and "To whom should I give this?"
   - **Test:** If you can replace the word with "he/she," use **who**. If you can replace it with "him/her," use **whom**.

3. **Phonetic Contraction ("Should of")**
   - **Error:** "We should of checked the data."
   - **Correct:** "We should have checked the data."
   - **Test:** "Of" is a preposition. It cannot follow "should," "could," or "would." The error stems from mishearing the spoken contraction "should've."

4. **Quantifier Drift ("Less" vs. "Fewer")**
   - **Error:** "We had less attendees this year."
   - **Correct:** "We had fewer attendees this year."
   - **Test:** If you can count the items individually (attendees, bugs, days), use "fewer." If it is a mass quantity (water, time, money), use "less."

5. **Transitive vs. Intransitive Verbs ("Lay" vs. "Lie")**
   - **Error:** "I need to go lay down."
   - **Correct:** "I need to go lie down."
   - **Test:** You "lay" an object down (e.g., lay the book on the table). You "lie" yourself down.

6. **Plural Contraction ("There's")**
   - **Error:** "There's three problems with this code."
   - **Correct:** "There are three problems with this code."
   - **Test:** Unpack the contraction. You would not say "There is three problems."

### WHY
These specific constructions sound correct to native speakers for two reasons. First, schools heavily drill subject rules (like "John and I went to the store"), causing writers to over-apply the rule and incorrectly use "I" when the pronoun is the object. Second, spoken English relies on phonetic blending ("should've"), which translates directly into incorrect written prepositions ("should of").

### COUNTER
Strictly enforcing these prescriptive rules harms communication in modern settings. Insisting on "To whom did you speak?" or correcting "There's a lot of people here" disrupts natural conversational flow. Descriptivist linguistics proves language is defined by actual use, not rigid textbooks. If most educated speakers say "Who did you send it to?", treating it as a mistake is pedantic and ignores how English syntax actually functions today. 

### CONDITIONS
Enforce these rules in academic writing, legal contracts, executive summaries, and formal publications. Write how people speak and ignore these strict rules in internal chats, casual emails, and verbal presentations.

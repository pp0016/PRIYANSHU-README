## What Happened to Your Creativity

Your creative ability wasn't a magic skill — it was built on **loaded context**. You had lots of niche knowledge in your head, and creativity was your brain combining those pieces in new ways. Two things killed it:

### 1. The Context Was Never Actually Stored

This is the hard truth. There's a difference between **exposure** and **encoding**.

- **Exposure** = you watched/read it, it felt familiar, you could use it while it was fresh
- **Encoding** = it's stored in long-term memory and retrievable weeks later

If you consumed that niche knowledge through passive scrolling/watching without active recall (testing yourself, writing about it, explaining it to someone), most of it was **never saved**. It lived in working memory temporarily, felt like knowledge, and then decayed.

This is called the **illusion of competence** (Bjork & Bjork, UCLA) — consumption feels like learning but doesn't produce durable memory. You felt creative because you had fresh context loaded. Once it faded, the raw material for creativity disappeared with it.

### 2. Your Brain Never Gets Idle Time Anymore

Creativity doesn't happen during consumption. It happens during **boredom**.

The **Default Mode Network** (Raichle, Washington University, 2001) is a brain network that activates when you're doing nothing — showering, walking, staring at a wall. This is the network that makes unexpected connections between stored ideas. It's literally the creativity engine.

**Constant screen input suppresses the DMN.** If every idle moment is filled with scrolling, your brain never enters the mode where creative mixing happens. The "shower thought" or "idea that hits while walking" stops happening because you're never mentally idle.

## Why This Isn't a Permanent Loss

Your brain didn't lose the ABILITY to be creative. It lost:
1. The **raw materials** (context that was never properly stored)
2. The **idle processing time** (DMN suppressed by constant input)

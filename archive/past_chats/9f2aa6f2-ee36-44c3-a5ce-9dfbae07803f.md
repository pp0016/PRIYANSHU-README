### 🤖 Antigravity
FFmpeg doesn't actually have a built-in "bulk images provision" with a hardcoded limit. Instead, the number of images you can process or compress in bulk depends entirely on **how** you are doing it and your computer's hardware. 

Here is how it breaks down depending on your goal:

### 1. Batch Compressing/Converting Individual Images
If you want to compress hundreds or thousands of images (e.g., converting a folder of `.png` to compressed `.jpg`), FFmpeg handles this one file at a time using a script or command-line loop (like a PowerShell or Bash `for` loop). 
* **The Limit:** There is **no limit**. You can point a script at a folder with 100,000 images, and it will process them sequentially one after the other until it finishes.
* **Concurrent Limit (At the exact same time):** If you want to run multiple FFmpeg compressions simultaneously to speed things up, the limit is based on your **CPU cores and RAM**. For example, on an 8-core processor, you could comfortably compress 6 to 8 images at the exact same time using a parallel script.

### 2. Combining Images into a Video (Image Sequence)
If your goal is to take a bulk folder of images and compress them into a single video file or timelapse (using patterns like `image_%04d.jpg`), FFmpeg reads the sequence frame-by-frame.
* **The Limit:** There is theoretically **no limit** to the length of the sequence. 
* **Caveat:** If you try to process tens of thousands of extremely high-resolution images (like 4K or 8K RAW photos) at once, you might eventually hit a memory (RAM) bottleneck on your computer. In extreme cases, users break them down into smaller video chunks and then join the videos together.

Priyanshu, here is the complete breakdown of Harmonics and Power Quality, mapped exactly to your Unit 3 syllabus. 

### 1. Power Quality (The Foundation)
**What it is:** Good power quality means delivering a perfect, smooth sine wave at exactly 50Hz and exactly 230V (or 415V for 3-phase). 

**The Financial Cost of Poor PQ (Exam Question):**
If the sine wave gets distorted, companies lose money in three ways:
1. **Hardware damage:** Motors overheat and burn out due to negative sequence currents.
2. **Production downtime:** Sensitive PLCs and computers reboot randomly when voltage drops.
3. **Tariff penalties:** Utilities (like GUVNL) charge massive penalties if poor power quality (like bad power factor) pollutes the main grid.

### 2. Harmonics (The Core Concept)
A harmonic is an unwanted, extra electrical frequency riding on top of your standard 50Hz power. 
* 1st Harmonic (Fundamental) = 50Hz (This is the good power you want).
* 3rd Harmonic = 150Hz (3 × 50)
* 5th Harmonic = 250Hz (5 × 50)

When these extra frequencies mix with your 50Hz wave, they distort the perfect sine wave into a jagged, messy shape. 

**What causes them? (Write exactly these three in the exam):**
They are caused by **Non-Linear Loads**. A linear load (like an old incandescent bulb or a simple heater) draws current smoothly. A non-linear load chops the current into pieces to convert AC to DC or change speeds. 
Your syllabus highlights three specific culprits:
1. **VFDs (Variable Frequency Drives):** They chop the wave to control motor speed.
2. **Rectifiers:** They chop the wave to convert AC to DC.
3. **UPS Systems:** The internal switching creates massive distortion.

### 3. THD (Total Harmonic Distortion)
This is how we measure the damage. THD is the ratio of all the "bad" harmonic currents compared to the "good" fundamental current.

**The Formula:**
`THD = [ √(I₃² + I₅² + I₇² ...) / I₁ ] × 100%`
*(Where I₁ is the 50Hz current, and I₃, I₅ are the unwanted harmonic currents).*

### 5. Sags, Swells, and Unbalance (Definitions)
* **Voltage Sag (Dip):** The voltage drops by 10% to 90% for a few milliseconds. *Cause:* Starting a massive industrial motor (it pulls 6x normal current, draining voltage from everything else).
* **Voltage Swell:** The voltage spikes up for a few milliseconds. *Cause:* Suddenly turning off a massive load, leaving excess voltage in the system.
* **Voltage Unbalance:** In a 3-phase system, the voltages in the R, Y, and B phases are not exactly equal (e.g., 415V, 400V, 410V). *Effect:* (IEC 60034-26 standard) This causes severe overheating in 3-phase induction motors because it creates opposing magnetic fields inside the stator.

***

**The Counter-Argument (Where textbook theory fails in real audits):**
In exams, installing an Active Harmonic Filter is the perfect answer to THD. In real life, Active Filters consume a significant amount of power just to run their own electronics (switching losses). An auditor must calculate if the energy saved by cleaning the harmonics is actually greater than the energy the active filter consumes. If it isn't, a simple Passive Filter is the better financial choice.

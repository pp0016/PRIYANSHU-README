The search results on GitHub for "Segment Anything" combined with "inpaint" and "animation/video" highlight several prominent projects that leverage these technologies for video editing and object removal. Key repositories include:

*   **[Track-Anything](https://github.com/gaomingqi/Track-Anything):** A flexible tool based on Segment Anything, XMem, and E2FGVI. It allows for interactive video object tracking and segmentation, enabling downstream tasks like video inpainting and object removal[1].
*   **[CoCoCo (Video-Inpaint-Anything)](https://github.com/zibojia/COCOCO):** This project focuses on text-guided video inpainting, aiming for better consistency and controllability. It utilizes Segment Anything 2 (SAM2) and diffusion models[2][3].
*   **[Inpaint-Anything](https://github.com/geekyutao/Inpaint-Anything):** While primarily focused on image inpainting, this repository has been extended to support video by segmenting objects in the first frame and tracking them through the video for removal[4].
*   **[Sam2_tk_gui](https://github.com/GreatenAnoymous/Sam2_tk_gui):** A Tkinter-based GUI application specifically designed to use SAM2 for video processing, including object removal and inpainting via LAMA or stable-diffusion-based techniques[5].
*   **[Video Segmentation and Inpainting](https://github.com/nsourlos/frame_inpainting_and_video_creation):** A pipeline that uses LangSAM (Language-based Segmentation) and Stable Diffusion to segment and inpaint specific objects in video frames based on text prompts[6].

These repositories generally combine segmentation models (like SAM or SAM2) with tracking algorithms (like XMem or OSTrack) and generative inpainting models (like Stable Diffusion, LAMA, or E2FGVI) to achieve video-level editing[4][1][5].

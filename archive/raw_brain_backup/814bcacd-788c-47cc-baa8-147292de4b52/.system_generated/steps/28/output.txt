Recent research in 2025 and 2026 has significantly advanced the use of **layer dropout** (also known as stochastic depth) as a powerful tool for both training efficiency and accelerating LLM inference through early exiting[1][2][3].

### Key Developments (2025–2026)
*   **"Don't Drop Dropout" (ICML 2026):** A prominent study published in September 2026 re-evaluates the role of layer dropout in large-scale LLM training[2][4]. The authors demonstrate that when modern pre-training recipes are updated with optimal layer distribution, time schedules, and optimizer hyperparameters, layer dropout can:
    *   **Reduce Training Costs:** Achieve lower or similar validation loss while saving up to **25% of training FLOPs**[1][2].
    *   **Enable Efficient Inference:** Provide "elastic depth," which makes models robust to post-training optimizations like **early exit**, **intermediate-layer skipping**, and **self-speculative decoding**[5][2][3].
    *   **Speedup:** These techniques yield up to **1.5x inference speedup** with negligible accuracy loss[1][4].

*   **Advanced Dynamic Skipping:**
    *   **DASH (2025):** This framework treats layer skipping as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), allowing for input-aware dynamic computation paths that adapt based on the token or input characteristic, further optimizing latency in resource-constrained environments[6][7].
    *   **DiffSkip (ACL 2025):** This approach introduced "Differential Layer Skipping," which focuses on dynamically skipping Feed-Forward Network (FFN) blocks based on the difference in self-attention blocks, allowing the model to adjust its depth per token during continuous generation[8].

*   **Two-Dimensional Early Exit (2026):** Research from early 2026 introduced a 2D strategy that coordinates both **layer-wise** and **sentence-wise** exiting[9][10]. By processing inputs incrementally, this method achieved additional speedups of 1.4x–2.3x over standard optimal layer-wise early exiting for classification tasks[9][11].

### Summary of Techniques for Inference Speedup
| Technique | Primary Mechanism | Key Benefit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Early Exit** | Exiting at intermediate layers to avoid full model compute[12][13]. | Reduced latency for "easy" tokens[12][14]. |
| **Self-Speculative Decoding** | Using early exits as a "draft" and remaining layers to verify[12][15]. | Parallelizes verification, reducing generation time[12][16]. |
| **Dynamic Layer Skipping** | Input-aware selection of compute paths (e.g., DASH, DiffSkip)[6][8]. | Efficient resource allocation per token[6][8]. |

### **Key Considerations for Local Inference**

#### **1. Total vs. Active Parameters (MoE Architecture)**
Both Llama 4 and newer Qwen3 models utilize **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)** architectures. 
*   **The Trap:** While a model like Llama 4 Maverick may only use 17B active parameters per token (making inference fast), it has 400B total parameters. **You must have enough VRAM to hold the entire 400B weight set.**
*   **Hardware Planning:** Always calculate VRAM needs based on the total parameter count to avoid out-of-memory (OOM) errors.

#### **2. Quantization and Context Window**
*   **4-bit Quantization (e.g., INT4, GGUF, AWQ):** This is the industry standard for consumer local inference. It generally requires ~0.5 GB of VRAM per 1 billion parameters.
*   **KV Cache:** This is the "hidden" memory consumer. As you increase the context window (e.g., Llama 4 Scout’s 10M token capability), the KV cache can grow from a few gigabytes to hundreds of gigabytes depending on the length and concurrent requests. For long-context tasks, memory requirements will scale far beyond the base weight size.

#### **3. Deployment Strategies**
*   **Consumer Hardware:** High-end consumer cards (RTX 3090/4090 with 24GB VRAM) are excellent for models up to 27B–32B. Running larger models (100B+) locally often requires multi-GPU setups (e.g., 4x or 8x H100s for production-grade inference) or significant usage of system RAM via Unified Memory architectures (e.g., high-capacity Mac Studios).
*   **Inference Engines:** Tools like `vLLM`, `llama.cpp`, and `Unsloth` are critical in 2026 for managing memory fragmentation and enabling quantization formats that make these large models accessible on less-than-enterprise-grade hardware.

### **Summary of 2026 Landscape**
*   **Llama 4** launched in 2025, with **Scout (109B)** and **Maverick (400B)** as the primary open-weight variants. They are designed for high-end research and production use cases rather than typical consumer desktop hardware.
*   **Qwen3 (specifically Qwen3.8)** is the latest major release (August 2026). It offers a range from smaller dense models (27B) to massive MoE variants (2.4T parameters) that require cluster-level hardware to host.

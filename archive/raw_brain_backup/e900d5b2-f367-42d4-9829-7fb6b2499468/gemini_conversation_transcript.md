## Gemini Response 2
Pain assessment systematically classifies pain characteristics, gathers detailed patient history, and quantifies intensity using standardized outcome measures.

**Classification & Types of Pain**

* **Somatic Pain:** Originates from musculoskeletal structures (skin, fascia, muscles, joints, tendons, bone). Typically well-localized, aching, or sharp, and directly reproducible with movement or mechanical loading.
* **Somatic Referred Pain:** Pain perceived in a region distant from its actual musculoskeletal origin (e.g., gluteal pain originating from lumbar facet joints). It occurs due to convergence of nociceptive afferents on second-order neurons in the spinal cord. It is dull and aching, lacks dermatomal distribution, and does not involve neurological deficits.
* **Neurogenic (Neuropathic) Pain:** Caused by primary lesion or dysfunction in the peripheral or central nervous system. Characterized by burning, shooting, electric shock-like sensations, often accompanied by numbness, paresthesia, or allodynia in a dermatomal/peripheral nerve pattern.
* **Visceral Pain:** Originates from internal organs (e.g., heart, gallbladder, kidneys). Poorly localized, deep, cramping, or aching, and often accompanied by autonomic responses (nausea, sweating, pallor). Pain may refer to predictable somatic sites (e.g., left shoulder pain during myocardial infarction).

**Subjective Pain Assessment**

* **Location & Distribution:** Specific site of pain, mapped out precisely to identify whether it follows a localized, dermatomal, or visceral pattern.
* **Duration & Progression:** Onset (acute, subacute, chronic), sequence of development over time, and whether symptoms are improving, static, or worsening.
* **Quality & Nature:** Specific descriptive terms used by the patient (e.g., throbbing, burning, sharp, dull, aching, stabbing) to help differentiate tissue mechanisms.
* **Diurnal Variations:** Symptom fluctuations over a 24-hour cycle (e.g., morning stiffness in inflammatory arthritis vs. end-of-day pain in mechanical loading).
* **Modifying Factors:** Specific activities, postures, movements, or interventions that aggravate or relieve symptoms.
* **Severity & Tissue Irritability:** Degree of pain intensity combined with how easily symptoms are triggered (vigor of movement needed), how severe they get, and how long they take to subside (return to baseline).

**Objective Measurement & Documentation**

* **Body Charts:** Visual anatomical maps where patients shade or mark pain locations using standard symbols (e.g., '#' for aching, '//' for sharp, '0' for numbness) to document distribution, depth, and spatial patterns.
* **Visual Analogue Scale (VAS):** A 100mm continuous horizontal line anchored by "No Pain" (0 mm) and "Worst Possible Pain" (100 mm). The patient marks a point, and the distance is measured in millimeters for precise tracking.
* **Numerical Rating Scale (NRS):** An 11-point numerical scale (0 to 10) where patients verbally rate their pain intensity, with 0 representing "no pain" and 10 representing "worst pain imaginable."
* **McGill Pain Questionnaire (MPQ / Modified MPQ):** A multidimensional rating tool evaluating sensory, affective, and evaluative dimensions of pain using grouped word descriptors, along with a present pain intensity (PPI) index.

**Subjective Examination**

* **Past Medical/Surgical History:** Prior myocardial infarction, hypertension, asthma/COPD, rheumatic fever, cardiac catheterization, cardiothoracic surgeries, or prolonged immobilizations.
* **Family History:** Early-onset coronary artery disease, cardiomyopathy, congenital heart disease, asthma, or cystic fibrosis.
* **Occupational & Environmental History:** Occupational inhalants (asbestos, silica, coal dust, organic molds) and home exposure (passive smoke, biomass fuel, environmental allergens).

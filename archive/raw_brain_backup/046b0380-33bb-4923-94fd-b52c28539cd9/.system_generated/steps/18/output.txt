### The Reality of 8GB VRAM in 2026
*   **The "Context" Bottleneck:** Even if you can fit a model into memory, you will likely hit "Out of Memory" (OOM) errors or severe performance degradation when trying to use large context windows (e.g., 32k+ tokens). You are often limited to smaller context sizes, which restricts the model's ability to handle long documents, complex codebases, or extended agentic workflows.
*   **Inference vs. Training:** 8GB is sufficient for **inference** (running existing models) if you are selective. However, **fine-tuning or training** is largely impractical on 8GB cards without advanced, highly technical optimizations (like dynamic batch size controllers or heavy quantization), and even then, it is prone to instability.
*   **Performance Stutters:** If your model and its KV cache exceed 8GB, the system will spill data into your slower system RAM. This causes a massive drop in tokens-per-second, turning a fast, interactive experience into one that crawls.

### What You Can Actually Do
Despite the limitations, users report success with specific, optimized workflows:
*   **LLMs:** Small, highly efficient models (typically in the 4B–8B parameter range, such as **Phi-4 Mini** or specialized **Qwen** variants) work well when quantized (Q4_K_M). These can deliver usable speeds for chat and basic logic tasks.
*   **Image Generation:** You can comfortably run **Stable Diffusion 1.5** and **SDXL** with optimized settings (e.g., via ComfyUI or Fooocus). Newer models like **Flux.1 Schnell** are often "workable" if you use GGUF quantization.
*   **Avoid "FOMO":** Many seasoned users advise against buying new, expensive hardware just to chase the latest models. The field moves so fast that today's "must-have" specs are often eclipsed quickly. Using cloud-based APIs for heavy lifting while reserving your 8GB card for learning and local, lightweight tasks is often recommended as the most cost-effective path.

### Summary of Recommendations

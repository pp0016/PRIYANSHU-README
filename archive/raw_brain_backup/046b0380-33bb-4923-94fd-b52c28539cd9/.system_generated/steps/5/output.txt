### **Key Strategies for Local Deployment**
To run these models on consumer hardware, you should avoid "raw" loading and use the following methods:

*   **Quantization (FP8 / GGUF):** This is the most effective way to reduce VRAM usage. For instance, the Wan 14B model can drop from ~65GB (FP16) to as little as 6GB (GGUF 480p)[1][2].
*   **CPU Offloading:** Interfaces like **ComfyUI** allow you to offload parts of the model (like the text encoder) to your system RAM. This significantly lowers the GPU VRAM floor at the cost of slower generation speeds[1][5].
*   **Tiling & Slicing:** For models like CogVideoX, using VAE tiling and memory-efficient attention (often built into ComfyUI custom nodes) prevents "Out of Memory" (OOM) errors during high-resolution or long-duration renders[6][2].
*   **Resolution and Framerate:** VRAM usage scales quadratically with resolution and linearly with frame count. If you encounter OOM errors, reduce the resolution (e.g., from 720p to 480p) or frame rate (e.g., from 16fps to 8fps) and use a tool like RIFE or a frame interpolator afterward to restore smoothness[1][7][8].

### **Hardware Recommendations**
*   **Minimum (Entry-level):** 8–12 GB VRAM (e.g., RTX 3060 12GB, 4060 Ti 16GB). Sufficient for Wan 1.3B or optimized CogVideoX 2B[1][9][2].
*   **Recommended (Sweet Spot):** 24 GB VRAM (e.g., RTX 3090, 4090). This is the gold standard for local AI video, allowing you to run 14B models with much higher quality and faster inference times[1][4][8].
*   **System RAM:** Since many workflows involve CPU offloading, **32GB to 64GB of system RAM** is highly recommended to prevent system-wide freezes during model loading[4][10][11].

**Pro Tip:** Use **ComfyUI** with community-maintained wrappers (e.g., `CogVideoXWrapper` or `WanVideoWrapper`). These nodes are specifically optimized for local memory management and provide the most stable way to run these models without needing a datacenter GPU[3][12][11].

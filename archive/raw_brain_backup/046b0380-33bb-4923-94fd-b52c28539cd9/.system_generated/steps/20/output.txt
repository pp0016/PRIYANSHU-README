### **Stable Diffusion 1.5**
*   **4 GB (Minimum):** You can run SD 1.5 on 4 GB of VRAM[5][3]. It is lightweight and remains the most accessible model for older hardware[5].
*   **Performance:** At 4 GB, you may need to use "low-VRAM" or "med-VRAM" optimization flags in your UI (like Automatic1111 or ComfyUI) to prevent out-of-memory errors[10]. 
*   **Recommendation:** While it *can* run on 4 GB, **8 GB or more** is recommended for a smoother experience, especially if you plan to use multiple LoRAs, ControlNet, or larger batch sizes[6][3].

### **SDXL (Stable Diffusion XL)**
*   **6–8 GB (Minimum):** SDXL is significantly more demanding than 1.5[11][4]. While it can technically run on 6–8 GB of VRAM, this is often considered the "danger zone"[11][8].
    *   You will almost certainly need to use memory-saving optimizations (such as `medvram` or `lowvram` modes)[12][8][3].
    *   Generation will be significantly slower, and you may encounter crashes if you attempt to use the Refiner model, complex ControlNet pipelines, or high-resolution upscaling[7][13][8].
*   **12 GB–16 GB (Recommended):** This is the "practical floor" for modern SDXL workflows[1][7][3]. 
    *   **12 GB** allows for a comfortable experience, including the use of LoRAs and single ControlNet passes[7][3].
    *   **16 GB** is ideal for using the full SDXL refiner ensemble and ensures you won't be constantly fighting memory constraints[9][7].

### **Tips for Limited VRAM (4 GB - 8 GB)**
*   **Use ComfyUI:** Many users find that ComfyUI is more efficient with VRAM than other interfaces, making it the preferred choice for those on 4 GB–6 GB cards[8][14].
*   **Optimization Flags:** Always look for and enable memory-saving settings (e.g., `--medvram`, `--lowvram`, or model offloading) in your web UI[15][10][12].
*   **Precision Matters:** Using models in **FP8** (if supported by your UI/model) instead of FP16 can significantly reduce the VRAM footprint[5][7].
*   **Cloud Alternatives:** If your hardware is consistently causing errors or taking too long, consider using cloud-based GPU services (like RunPod or similar) where you can rent powerful hardware by the hour[6][3].

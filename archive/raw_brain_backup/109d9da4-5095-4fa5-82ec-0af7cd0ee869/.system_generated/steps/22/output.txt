Thunderbolt 5 and Thunderbolt 4 differ significantly in their PCIe tunneling capabilities and total bandwidth, marking a major leap for high-performance peripherals like external GPUs and NVMe storage[1][2][3].

### **PCIe Tunneling & Bandwidth Comparison**

| Feature | Thunderbolt 4 | Thunderbolt 5 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Total Bidirectional Bandwidth** | 40 Gbps[2][3] | 80 Gbps[2][3] |
| **Bandwidth Boost (Display)** | N/A | Up to 120 Gbps[2][3] |
| **PCIe Data Bandwidth** | **32 Gbps**[2][3] | **64 Gbps**[2][3] |
| **PCIe Generation** | PCIe 3.0[1][4] | PCIe 4.0[1][5] |

### **Key Technical Differences**

*   **Thunderbolt 4 (32 Gbps PCIe):**
    *   Thunderbolt 4 mandates a minimum of 32 Gbps of PCIe data bandwidth out of its total 40 Gbps link[6][7]. 
    *   It relies on **PCIe 3.0 x4** tunneling[1][4].
    *   While sufficient for most general-purpose tasks and mid-range external storage (typically hitting ~3,000 MB/s), it often becomes a bottleneck for high-end external GPUs and the latest Gen 4 or Gen 5 NVMe SSDs[1][8][9].

*   **Thunderbolt 5 (64 Gbps PCIe):**
    *   Thunderbolt 5 doubles the dedicated PCIe bandwidth to **64 Gbps** by utilizing **PCIe 4.0 x4** tunneling[2][10][3].
    *   This upgrade allows for much higher real-world performance for external storage, with benchmarks showing speeds jumping from the ~3,000 MB/s ceiling of Thunderbolt 4 to upwards of 6,000–6,500 MB/s[11][9].
    *   The architecture is designed to handle more demanding workflows, such as high-refresh-rate multi-monitor setups (up to 3x 4K at 144Hz) and more capable external GPU enclosures, by providing significantly more "headroom" for data traffic[2][12][13].

### **Summary of Impact**
The shift from 32 Gbps to 64 Gbps is primarily aimed at eliminating bottlenecks for professional and enthusiast hardware[1][5]. While Thunderbolt 4 remains a robust standard for office docks and standard peripherals, Thunderbolt 5 is engineered specifically for users who require the full throughput of PCIe 4.0 storage and more powerful external graphics solutions[1][3][13].

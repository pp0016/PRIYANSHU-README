Apple's **UltraFusion** interconnect technology is implemented using **TSMC’s InFO_LI (Integrated Fan-Out with Local Silicon Interconnect)** packaging method[1][2][3].

While there was initial industry speculation that the M1 Ultra might use TSMC’s CoWoS-S (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate with silicon interposer)[1][2], it was later clarified that Apple utilized **InFO_LI** to achieve the necessary chip-to-chip bandwidth at a lower cost and complexity[1][2].

### Key Technical Details:
*   **Bridge vs. Interposer:** Rather than using a full, large-scale silicon interposer (like in CoWoS-S), UltraFusion utilizes a **passive silicon bridge** (the Local Silicon Interconnect or LSI)[1][3]. This bridge sits beneath the two M1 Max dies to facilitate high-speed, high-density communication[1][2][3].
*   **How it Works:** The InFO_LI process allows Apple to connect two M1 Max dies so that they function as a single, unified processor[4][5]. This architecture provides 2.5 TB/s of low-latency inter-processor bandwidth[1][4]. 
*   **Comparison:** This approach is functionally similar to Intel’s **EMIB (Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge)**, as both rely on localized silicon bridges to connect chiplets rather than requiring a single, massive silicon interposer covering the entire package area[1][6].
*   **Evolution:** The technology continues to be used in subsequent generations, with recent reports indicating that newer iterations (such as the M5 Ultra) utilize nested UltraFusion packaging based on InFO-LSI technology to connect multiple dies[7].

Ping latency over a **Thunderbolt Bridge** on macOS is typically measured in **sub-millisecond** values[1].

### Typical Latency Values
*   **Average Latency:** Approximately **0.3 ms** (or ~300 µs)[1].
*   **Performance Range:** Reported round-trip times (RTT) for Thunderbolt networking are often in the range of **0.1 ms to 0.5 ms**[1].

### Key Context
*   **Comparison:** This is significantly faster than standard network connections like Wi-Fi, which often exhibit latencies of 20 ms to 70 ms or higher depending on congestion[1][2].
*   **Usage:** Because of this extremely low latency, Thunderbolt Bridge is highly effective for tasks requiring fast communication between two Macs, such as distributed computing, high-performance file sharing, or low-latency remote display/control[3][4][1].
*   **Technical Note:** While standard IP-over-Thunderbolt (Thunderbolt Bridge) provides these low latencies, newer macOS versions (starting with macOS 26.2 on Apple silicon Macs with Thunderbolt 5) also support **RDMA over Thunderbolt**, which can offer even tighter, more direct communication for specialized high-performance clustering tasks[5].

If you are experiencing higher latency, ensure that your network traffic is actually routing through the Thunderbolt interface (you can check your Service Order in **System Settings > Network**) and that you are using a certified Thunderbolt-compatible cable, as some standard USB-C cables may not support the full bandwidth or latency benefits[6][7][8].

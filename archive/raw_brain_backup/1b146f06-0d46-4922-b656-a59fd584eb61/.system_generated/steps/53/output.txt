Transitioning from a MacBook to a Mac Mini is a common shift for users looking for a more permanent, desk-bound setup. While the experience is largely similar to using a MacBook, the primary "difficulty" new users encounter is the requirement to provide their own peripherals—and ensuring they connect properly during the initial setup.

### Common Setup Hurdles
*   **Initial Peripheral Pairing:** Unlike a MacBook, where the keyboard and trackpad are built-in, a Mac Mini requires external input devices. If you are using a wireless keyboard or mouse that isn't Apple-branded, it may not connect automatically during the initial setup screens.
*   **The "Power Button" Trick:** If your Bluetooth keyboard or mouse is not being detected during setup, users recommend pressing the Mac Mini’s power button three times. This often triggers a Bluetooth pairing prompt on the screen.
*   **The "Emergency" Wired Keyboard:** Many experienced Mac Mini users keep a cheap, wired USB keyboard and mouse on hand. Even if you plan to use wireless peripherals, having a wired set can be a lifesaver if the machine fails to pair with your wireless devices during the setup or if you ever need to perform an internet recovery.
*   **Dongles and Adapters:** If you are reusing peripherals from a PC or older setup, ensure you have the necessary USB-A to USB-C adapters. The Mac Mini has limited "traditional" USB-A ports (depending on the model), so plan your connections accordingly.

### Migration Tips (Moving from MacBook)
*   **Use Migration Assistant:** This is the most reliable way to move your data. Connect your MacBook and Mac Mini (via Wi-Fi or, ideally, an Ethernet/USB cable for faster speeds) and run the Migration Assistant when prompted during the initial startup. It will transfer your apps, documents, and system settings seamlessly.
*   **Time Machine Backup:** If you want a stress-free migration, create a full Time Machine backup of your MacBook to an external drive. You can then plug that drive into your new Mac Mini and select "Restore from Time Machine" during the setup process.
*   **Embrace the Desktop Experience:** You are moving from a device with a built-in display to one that requires an external one. Some users find that standard 4K monitors may require a bit of tinkering with macOS display settings (specifically HiDPI/scaling) to ensure text and icons don't appear "teeny tiny." Using a high-quality cable (e.g., a modern 8K-rated HDMI or Thunderbolt cable) can often resolve flickering or resolution issues.

### Frequently Asked Questions
*   **Can I use my MacBook as a monitor?** Generally, no—you cannot use a MacBook as a direct external display for a Mac Mini (via a cable). You would need third-party software for "screen sharing" or a dedicated monitor.
*   **Do I need a KVM switch?** If you plan to switch between your new Mac Mini and a work laptop frequently, a KVM switch or a high-quality docking station is often recommended to avoid constant cable swapping. Alternatively, Apple’s built-in Screen Sharing (Remote Desktop) can allow you to control one Mac from another without extra hardware.

**Bottom line:** If you get stuck during setup, **don't panic**. A wired USB keyboard is the "universal key" to solving almost any initial connection hurdle. Once you are through the initial setup, the macOS experience will feel exactly like the MacBook you are already comfortable with.

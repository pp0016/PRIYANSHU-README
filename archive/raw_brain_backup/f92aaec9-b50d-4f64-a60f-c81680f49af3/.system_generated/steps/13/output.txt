### **Key Trends & Context (2025–2026)**

*   **The "Output Premium":** A critical factor in current billing is the ratio between input and output costs. At most providers, **output tokens cost 5x to 6x more than input tokens**. Because models generate more tokens in verbose responses, your actual bill is often driven more by *what the model writes* than *what you send it*.
*   **Price War & Volatility:** The market has moved toward aggressive, mid-quarter price cuts. For example, OpenAI reduced rates for several tiers in July 2026, while Anthropic adjusted its "introductory" pricing for Claude Sonnet 5 to "standard" pricing on September 1, 2026.
*   **Beyond the "Headline" Price:** 
    *   **Context Caching:** Most providers now offer cached input pricing (typically 10–20% of the base input rate), which significantly reduces costs for long-running agentic workflows that reuse the same context.
    *   **Outcome-Based Pricing:** Many enterprise platforms are moving away from pure per-token billing toward "cost per completed task" (e.g., cost per resolved support ticket), which aligns vendor costs more closely with business value.
*   **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO):** Analysts emphasize that while per-token prices have fallen 40–60% since 2025, total enterprise AI spending is rising. This is due to the deployment of more complex, agentic workflows that require more "thinking" tokens and frequent API calls, often causing companies to spend 2–3x their initial engineering forecasts.

**Recommendation:** Do not rely on headline rates for budget planning. Because providers bill differently for reasoning tokens, context windows, and cached data, the best practice in 2026 is to benchmark costs based on your specific **workload volume** and **average task completion cost** rather than unit token price.

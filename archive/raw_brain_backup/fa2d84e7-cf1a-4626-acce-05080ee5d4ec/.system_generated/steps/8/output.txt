NVIDIA’s mobile and consumer GPU roadmap is currently defined by an **annual architecture cadence** that prioritizes AI acceleration and high-bandwidth memory (HBM/GDDR7)[1][2][3]. While datacenter roadmaps are strictly defined (Blackwell $\rightarrow$ Rubin $\rightarrow$ Rubin Ultra $\rightarrow$ Feynman), consumer laptop GPU VRAM trends are heavily influenced by memory supply constraints and a shifting industry focus toward AI-integrated SoCs[1][4][5].

### GPU Architecture & Release Roadmap (2027–2029)
NVIDIA has moved to an annual release cycle for its architectures[1][2]. Following the Blackwell generation (shipping now), the trajectory is as follows:

*   **2027:** The **Rubin Ultra** architecture is expected to serve as the high-end standard for enterprise and workstation systems, focusing on HBM4 memory and optical interconnects[1][6][7]. For consumer laptops, expectations are centered on the **RTX 60 series** (codenamed "Ruby" or "GR200"), which is anticipated to debut in late 2027 or early 2028 due to extended development cycles and memory supply limitations[8][4][5].
*   **2028:** The **Feynman** architecture is slated for release, introducing advanced 3D stacking and custom memory technologies[1][2]. This generation will likely emphasize higher compute density for AI and next-gen gaming features[9][2].
*   **2029–2030:** The **Rosa Feynman** generation is the projected successor, continuing the push for integrated AI-processing power[1].

### VRAM & Laptop Memory Trends
The trend for laptop VRAM is increasingly tied to the distinction between discrete GPUs and "Superchip" SoCs (like the RTX Spark platform)[10][11].

*   **The 16GB/24GB "Standard":** 
    *   **16GB** is currently establishing itself as the "sweet spot" for mid-to-high-range gaming laptops (e.g., RTX 5070-class) to ensure longevity for modern AAA titles and AI workloads[12][13].
    *   **24GB** is emerging as the new high-end standard for flagship laptop GPUs (e.g., RTX 5090 Mobile), mirroring the capacity previously reserved for workstation cards[13]. 
    *   **Constraints:** Industry reports indicate that memory supply constraints have occasionally forced NVIDIA to pull back on higher-VRAM "Super" refreshes, making it difficult to guarantee that 24GB will become a universal baseline across all mid-range segments in the immediate future[14][5][15].
*   **Unified Memory (RTX Spark):** A significant shift for 2027–2029 is the **RTX Spark** platform, which utilizes a unified memory architecture[10]. These systems offer significantly higher memory ceilings—up to **128GB of LPDDR5X/LPDDR6 unified memory**—by integrating the GPU, CPU, and memory into a single package[16][17][11].

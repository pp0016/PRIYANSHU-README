The NVIDIA RTX 5060 laptop GPU, featuring 8GB of VRAM, has been a significant point of contention throughout 2025 and 2026. The criticism centers on whether 8GB is sufficient for the evolving demands of both modern gaming and local AI workloads[1][2][3].

### The Core Criticism: VRAM Limitations
*   **Gaming Performance:** While the RTX 5060 is widely regarded as a capable "1080p monster," its 8GB VRAM capacity is frequently criticized as a bottleneck for higher resolutions (1440p) and modern AAA titles[4][5][6]. Critics argue that modern games are increasingly memory-intensive, and an 8GB limit often leads to frame-time instability, stutters, and the need to lower texture settings to avoid performance hits[7][6][8].
*   **"Future-Proofing" Concerns:** Many reviewers and users argue that 8GB is insufficient for a mid-range card in the 2025–2026 market[2][9]. The concern is that as game engines become more complex, the card will prematurely age, forcing users to rely heavily on upscaling technologies like DLSS or compromise significantly on visual fidelity[10][8][11].
*   **AI Workloads:** For users interested in local AI (e.g., Large Language Models or image generation), 8GB of VRAM is considered an entry-level starting point[12]. While it is technically capable of running smaller models and performing basic tasks, it severely limits the size, complexity, and precision of the models that can be run locally[13][14][15]. Professionals or serious hobbyists often require more VRAM for larger datasets and faster inference[13][15].

### The Counter-Perspective
*   **Practical Use Cases:** Supporters of the RTX 5060 emphasize that for the intended market—1080p gaming and general-purpose performance—the GPU remains highly effective[16][5]. Proponents argue that "fear-mongering" about VRAM ignores the fact that at 1080p, 8GB remains largely adequate for the vast majority of titles[5][10].
*   **AI Flexibility:** Some observers note that through "quantization" and optimized software frameworks, an 8GB card can still function as an "AI workhorse" for many common local tasks, such as running smaller LLMs or Stable Diffusion, provided the user has a realistic understanding of the hardware's limits[12].

### Summary for 2025/2026
Whether the RTX 5060 is "future-proof" depends on your expectations:
*   **Buy it if:** You are a casual-to-mid-range gamer primarily playing at 1080p, or you want a budget-friendly laptop that can handle basic AI tasks and coursework[16][17][6].
*   **Avoid it/Look elsewhere if:** You plan on gaming at 1440p or higher, prefer playing "Ultra" settings in the latest AAA titles, or are specifically looking to invest in a machine for serious local AI development/training, where VRAM capacity is the primary constraint[4][6][15].

### Impact on Laptop GPU VRAM
The high cost and limited supply of GDDR7 memory have created a complex situation for laptop GPU configurations:

*   **VRAM Capacity Limitations:** While GDDR7 offers superior bandwidth and efficiency[9][10], its high cost acts as a barrier to increasing VRAM capacities in mainstream and mid-range laptops[7][8]. Manufacturers are often forced to balance between offering higher VRAM and maintaining affordable price points[7][11].
*   **Strategic Allocation:** In some cases, vendors have used the transition to newer memory modules (such as 3GB GDDR7 dies instead of 2GB dies) to offer higher capacity options, like 12GB variants for specific laptop GPUs (e.g., RTX 5070 laptop)[11]. However, these remain exceptions rather than the industry norm, and such moves are often limited by the overall scarcity of memory modules[11][12].
*   **Performance vs. Capacity:** Because the cost of adding more VRAM modules is significant, many laptop GPUs continue to utilize 8GB to 12GB of VRAM[13][14][11]. While GDDR7’s higher bandwidth helps mitigate some performance bottlenecks[13][10], users looking for large VRAM buffers (16GB+) in laptops will likely continue to face a "premium" tax or limited availability through 2027 due to these supply-chain constraints[7][12].

**Summary for Consumers:** If you are planning to purchase a laptop, do not expect a significant drop in prices or a widespread increase in VRAM capacities through 2027[2].

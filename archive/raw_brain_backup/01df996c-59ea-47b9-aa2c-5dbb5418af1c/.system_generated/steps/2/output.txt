### Detailed Comparison

#### 1. Gabb (Best for Absolute Restriction)
Gabb operates as a "digital wall"[5]. It is essentially a standard Android phone running a custom operating system (GabbOS) that completely removes the internet browser, app store, and social media[5][9]. 
*   **School-Friendly:** Because it lacks addictive feeds and open internet, it is a low-distraction device[11][15].
*   **Pros:** Simplest to manage; no "workarounds" for kids; high-quality hardware (Samsung-based)[5][9].
*   **Cons:** Very limited; may feel "babyish" to older teens who need specific apps for school or extracurriculars[16][9].

#### 2. Pinwheel (Best for Incremental Growth)
Pinwheel is designed to grow with your child[10]. It uses a modified Android environment that allows parents to curate a specific list of safe apps (from 1,200+ vetted options)[12][13].
*   **School-Friendly:** You can set "lockout" schedules for specific apps or contacts based on the time of day, making it easy to restrict usage during school hours[12][4].
*   **Pros:** More flexible than Gabb; gives parents the ability to slowly introduce useful tools (like maps or calendars) without opening the door to the "open" web[7][10].

#### 3. Bark Phone (Best for Monitoring & Older Teens)
The Bark Phone is a full-featured Samsung smartphone with robust, tamper-proof parental controls and AI-driven monitoring built directly into the system[5][8].
*   **School-Friendly:** Parents can manage app access and screen time remotely[8]. It provides the most "real-world" experience, allowing for social media apps *if* you choose to enable them, while still alerting you to red-flag behaviors like cyberbullying or self-harm[8][17].
*   **Pros:** Best for older teens who need a "normal" phone for social/school reasons but still require safety oversight[1][8].

### Summary Recommendation
*   **For ages 8–12:** **Gabb** is often the easiest choice because it requires the least amount of management from parents[1].
*   **For "in-between" ages (tweens):** **Pinwheel** is excellent if you want to selectively allow apps (like a calculator, music, or school-related tools) without allowing open browsing[10].
*   **For teens (14+):** **The Bark Phone** is typically the best fit, as it allows them to have a functional smartphone while providing you with detailed insights into their digital health and safety[8][18].

**Note:** If you find these dedicated phones too restrictive or expensive, remember that you can also set up a standard iPhone or Android device using built-in parental controls (Apple Screen Time or Google Family Link) for free, which may be a more cost-effective "school-friendly" solution for older children[1].

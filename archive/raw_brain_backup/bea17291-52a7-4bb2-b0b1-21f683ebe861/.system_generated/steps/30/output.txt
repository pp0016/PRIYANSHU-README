The command `rdma_ctl enable` is used on Apple silicon Macs with Thunderbolt 5 ports (running macOS 26.2 or later) to enable **RDMA (Remote Direct Memory Access) over Thunderbolt**[1][2][3].

This feature is designed to allow high-performance, low-latency, peer-to-peer communication between Mac computers, primarily for creating distributed computing clusters for tasks like local AI model inference[1][4][5].

### How to Enable RDMA
Because this is a specialized, early-stage feature, it must be enabled manually via the macOS Recovery environment[2][6]:

1.  **Enter Recovery Mode:** Shut down your Mac, then hold down the power button for approximately 10 seconds until the boot options menu appears[3][7].
2.  **Open Terminal:** Select **Options** to enter the Recovery environment, then navigate to **Utilities > Terminal** in the top menu bar[1][3].
3.  **Run the Command:** Type the following command and press Enter[8][3]:
    `rdma_ctl enable`
4.  **Reboot:** Restart your Mac to return to normal operation[8][9].

### Key Details & Caveats
*   **Requirements:** You must have a Mac with Apple silicon and Thunderbolt 5 ports, running at least macOS 26.2[1][3]. You also need compatible Thunderbolt 5 cables[8][3].
*   **Verification:** Once back in macOS, you can verify that the RDMA interfaces are active by running the command `ibv_devices` in your Terminal[4][9].
*   **Network Topology:** There are currently no Thunderbolt 5 switches available[10][6]. To connect a cluster of Macs, you must use a direct, full-mesh cabling configuration[11][10].
*   **Usage:** This feature is often used in conjunction with distributed AI frameworks like **MLX** (via the JACCL backend) or **Exo** to enable tensor parallelism across multiple Mac machines[2][12][13].

For official technical guidance, refer to **Apple Technote TN3205: Low-latency communication with RDMA over Thunderbolt**[1][9].

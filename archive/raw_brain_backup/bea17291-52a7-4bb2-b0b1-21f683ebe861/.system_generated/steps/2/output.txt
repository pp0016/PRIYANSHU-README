It is **not possible** to expand your Mac's physical RAM using an external Thunderbolt drive or any other external storage device.

RAM (Random Access Memory) and storage (SSD/HDD) serve fundamentally different purposes in your computer, and they cannot be swapped or combined in the way you might expect. Below is a breakdown of why this is the case and how your Mac actually manages memory.

### 1. RAM vs. Storage: The Fundamental Difference
*   **RAM (Memory):** Acts as your Mac’s "short-term workspace." It is extremely fast and has near-instantaneous latency, allowing the processor to access data immediately. It is soldered directly to the motherboard in modern Macs, meaning it cannot be upgraded after purchase.
*   **Storage (SSD):** Acts as your "long-term filing cabinet." Even the fastest Thunderbolt SSDs, while impressive, are orders of magnitude slower than physical RAM. They are designed to hold files and applications that persist even when the power is off.

### 2. How macOS Uses "Swap" Memory
You may have heard that your Mac uses storage when it runs out of RAM. This is called **Swap** (or Virtual Memory).
*   **Automatic Management:** When your physical RAM is full, macOS automatically moves inactive data from the RAM to your internal SSD to free up space for active tasks.
*   **Why you can't "expand" it:** You cannot manually assign an external drive to act as this "Swap" space to increase your total memory capacity. macOS manages swap files on the system's startup disk automatically. 
*   **Performance Impact:** Relying on Swap is a sign that your system is under memory pressure. Because SSDs are significantly slower than RAM, your Mac will feel sluggish, encounter spinning beach balls, or experience crashes if it has to rely heavily on Swap memory.

### 3. What You Can Do Instead
If your Mac is struggling with memory-intensive tasks:
*   **Close Background Apps:** Since you cannot add physical RAM, closing memory-heavy applications (like web browsers with many tabs, video editors, or virtual machines) is the most effective way to free up your existing RAM.
*   **Clear Storage Space:** Keep plenty of free space on your **internal** SSD. macOS needs that "headroom" to efficiently manage swap files and background processes. If your internal drive is nearly full, your system's performance will degrade significantly.
*   **Use External Storage for Files:** If your internal drive is full, move your large project files, photo libraries, or media collections to a fast **external Thunderbolt SSD**. This clears up your internal SSD, ensuring the system has plenty of space to breathe and perform its own memory management tasks effectively.
*   **Restart Daily:** Restarting your Mac regularly clears the cache and resets the memory, which can help if you notice the system becoming slow over time.

**Summary:** You cannot turn an external Thunderbolt disk into RAM. If your current Mac does not have enough memory for your professional needs, the only way to increase physical RAM is to use a device with more memory (e.g., upgrading to a model with higher unified memory).

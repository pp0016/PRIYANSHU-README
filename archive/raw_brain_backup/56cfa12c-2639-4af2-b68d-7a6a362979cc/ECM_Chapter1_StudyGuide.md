## TOPIC 1: ENERGY CONSERVATION ACT 2001

**Q: Discuss the salient features of Energy Conservation Act 2001. (7 marks)**

The Energy Conservation Act was enacted in October 2001 in India. Its primary objective is to reduce the energy intensity of the Indian economy. The government has notified a list of designated consumers in 9 sectors, BEE star labeling, and ECBC norms under this act. 

Key features of the Act are:
1. The Central Government can specify energy consumption standards for appliances and equipment.
2. It mandates standards and labeling for equipment and appliances.
3. Designated consumers (large energy users) must appoint an energy manager, get energy audits done, and comply with norms.
4. It established the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) under the Ministry of Power.
5. It defines penalties for non-compliance with the rules.
6. It introduced the Energy Conservation Building Code (ECBC) for commercial buildings.
7. State Designated Agencies (SDAs) are established for implementation at the state level.
8. The Central Government can prohibit the manufacture or sale of equipment not meeting standards.
9. It sets energy consumption standards for notified equipment.
10. It requires the mandatory display of energy consumption labels on equipment.

## TOPIC 2: BEE SCHEMES

**Q: Enlist the schemes of Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) and discuss any two in detail. (7 marks)**

The schemes of the Bureau of Energy Efficiency include:
1. Standards and Labeling Programme
2. Energy Conservation Building Code (ECBC)
3. Designated Consumers Programme
4. Demand Side Management (DSM)
5. Bachat Lamp Yojna
6. SME Energy Efficiency Programme
7. Agriculture DSM Programme
8. Municipal DSM Programme
9. National Mission on Enhanced Energy Efficiency (NMEEE)
10. Perform Achieve and Trade (PAT) Scheme

Standards and Labeling Programme provides star ratings for appliances based on their energy consumption. It helps consumers make informed choices by showing the energy efficiency of a product. Products with higher star ratings consume less electricity and save money over time.

Energy Conservation Building Code (ECBC) sets minimum energy performance standards for new commercial buildings. It applies to buildings with a connected load of 100 kW or more. It covers aspects like the building envelope, HVAC, and lighting to ensure the building consumes less energy compared to conventional designs.

**Q: Enlist various schemes of BEE and its benefits. (3 marks)**

The BEE schemes include the Standards and Labeling Programme, ECBC, PAT Scheme, Demand Side Management, and Bachat Lamp Yojna. These schemes help reduce energy consumption, lower electricity bills for consumers, and decrease the peak power demand on the grid. They also promote the use of energy-efficient appliances and sustainable building designs across the country.

## TOPIC 3: INDIAN ENERGY SCENARIO

**Q: Write a short note on Indian Energy Scenario. (7 marks)**
*(Note: For a 4-mark question "Discuss sector-wise energy consumption scenario in India", write only the first two paragraphs.)*

India is the third largest energy consumer globally after China and the USA. The primary energy sources heavily rely on coal which meets about 55% of the demand. Oil accounts for about 30% and natural gas for about 8%. Hydro, nuclear, and renewables make up the rest. Sector-wise consumption shows Industry at the top with roughly 45% usage. Transport uses about 18%, Residential 23%, Agriculture 8%, and Commercial 6%. Coal dominates power generation and produces about 70% of the country's electricity.

India faces a high import dependency. About 80% of crude oil and 50% of natural gas are imported. The growing population, rapid urbanization, and industrial growth continuously increase energy demand. 

Despite total high consumption, the per capita energy consumption is still low. India's per capita consumption is about 600 kgoe compared to the world average of 1800 kgoe. The government is heavily pushing for renewables to meet future demand. Initiatives like the National Solar Mission and a target of 500 GW of renewable energy capacity by 2030 are key steps in this direction.

## TOPIC 4: ENERGY SECURITY

**Q: Define energy security. Enlist different strategies to achieve it. (3 marks)**

Energy security means the continuous availability of energy in various forms, in sufficient quantities, and at reasonable prices. Strategies to achieve energy security include:
1. Diversifying energy sources.
2. Increasing domestic energy production.
3. Building strategic energy reserves.
4. Promoting renewable energy.
5. Implementing energy conservation measures.
6. Engaging in international energy cooperation.

**Q: Define energy security. Enlist strategies and discuss the role of energy conservation in achieving energy security. (7 marks)**

Energy security means the continuous availability of energy in various forms, in sufficient quantities, and at reasonable prices. Strategies to achieve it include diversifying energy sources, increasing domestic production, building strategic reserves, promoting renewables, implementing energy conservation, and international cooperation.

Energy conservation plays a major role in achieving energy security. It directly reduces the total energy demand of the country. Lower demand means the country needs to import fewer fossil fuels like oil and gas. This reduces the financial burden and improves economic stability. Conservation also extends the life of existing domestic fossil fuel reserves. Using less energy lowers environmental impact and greenhouse gas emissions.

## TOPIC 5: CLASSIFICATION OF ENERGY

**Q: Define: (i) Renewable Energy (ii) Non-Renewable Energy (iii) Per Capita Energy Consumption. (3 marks)**

Renewable Energy comes from sources that are naturally replenished. Examples include solar, wind, hydro, biomass, and geothermal energy.
Non-Renewable Energy comes from sources that get depleted and cannot be replenished in a human timescale. Examples include coal, oil, natural gas, and nuclear energy.
Per Capita Energy Consumption is the total energy consumed by a country divided by its population. India's per capita consumption is about 600 kgoe per year.

**Q: Differentiate between primary and secondary sources and give examples. (4 marks)**

Primary sources are found directly in nature and used without conversion. Examples include coal, crude oil, natural gas, solar radiation, and wind.
Secondary sources are derived from primary sources through a conversion process. Examples include electricity, petrol, diesel, LPG, and coke.

## TOPIC 6: ENERGY CONSERVATION AND ITS IMPORTANCE

**Q: Define Energy Conservation and explain its importance. (4 marks)**

Energy conservation means reducing energy consumption by using less energy for the same work output or getting more work from the same amount of energy.

The importance of energy conservation includes:
1. It reduces energy bills for industries and households.
2. It decreases dependence on imported fuels like oil and gas.
3. It reduces environmental pollution and greenhouse gas emissions.
4. It extends the life of existing energy resources.
5. It improves the competitiveness of Indian industry in the global market.
6. It reduces the need for new power plant construction.

Example calculation: Replacing a 100W incandescent bulb with a 10W LED saves 90W. For a factory with 1000 such bulbs running 10 hours a day, the annual saving is (1000 * 90 * 10 * 365) / 1000 = 3,28,500 kWh.

## TOPIC 7: ECBC CODE

**Q: Define ECBC and its significance in promoting energy-efficient building construction. (7 marks)**

ECBC stands for Energy Conservation Building Code. It was launched by BEE in 2007 and updated in 2017 as ECBC 2017. The code applies to commercial buildings with a connected load of 100 kW or more, or a contract demand of 120 kVA or more.

It covers 6 building systems. These are the building envelope, HVAC, lighting, electrical systems, water heating, and renewable energy. It sets minimum energy performance standards for new commercial buildings. There are three levels of compliance: ECBC as the baseline, ECBC+ for enhanced efficiency, and SuperECBC for near net-zero energy buildings. 

ECBC buildings consume 25 to 50 percent less energy than conventional buildings. States have the power to adopt and notify the ECBC with modifications to suit local climatic conditions. It is currently voluntary at the national level, but states like Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, UP, and Rajasthan have made it mandatory.

## TOPIC 8: ELECTRICITY ACT 2003

**Q: Discuss the provisions of Electricity Act 2003 related to energy conservation. (7 marks)**

The Electricity Act was enacted in 2003. It replaced the Indian Electricity Act 1910, the Electricity Supply Act 1948, and the Electricity Regulatory Commissions Act 1998. 

Section 3 states that the national electricity policy and plan must include provisions for energy conservation. Section 61 requires tariff regulations to promote efficiency in electricity use. Section 86(1)(e) directs state commissions to promote cogeneration and renewable energy sources. Section 166 advises state governments to promote energy efficiency.

The Act also delicensed power generation except for hydro projects above 25 MW and nuclear power. It introduced open access provisions to promote competition and reduce transmission losses. These provisions increased private participation, reduced transmission and distribution losses, and promoted competition.

## TOPIC 10: ENERGY PRICING

**Q: Explain in brief energy pricing. (3 marks)**

Energy pricing is the mechanism by which energy costs are determined for consumers. In India, energy prices are set through a mix of market forces and government regulation. Electricity tariffs are set by State Electricity Regulatory Commissions (SERCs) and vary by consumer category such as domestic, commercial, industrial, and agricultural. 

Petroleum product prices were deregulated between 2010 and 2014 and now follow international crude oil prices. Natural gas prices are set by the government every 6 months using a formula linked to international gas prices. Coal prices are set by Coal India Ltd with government oversight. Energy pricing directly affects conservation behavior because higher prices encourage energy saving. There is also cross-subsidization where industrial and commercial consumers pay higher rates to subsidize domestic and agricultural consumers.
